Why People Buy Abroad

為什麼人們要在海外買房

Four motives account for most cross-border residential purchases, and each deserves an honest look rather than a marketing slogan. Diversification — spreading exposure away from a single home country's property cycle, currency, and policy risk — is real, but a single foreign apartment is a concentrated, illiquid, undiversified bet on one city, not a diversified position; true diversification usually requires either many properties across many markets (rarely realistic for an individual) or the REIT alternative discussed at the end of this module. Children's education — buying near a target university or school district years in advance — is a genuine, common driver, but it prices a housing decision around a single family's timeline rather than the property's own investment merit, and the property often gets sold in a hurry the year the education need ends, regardless of market conditions. Migration hedging — buying a foothold in a country the owner might want to move to or send family to later — has real option value, but that value is highest exactly where legal purchase is hardest (countries with strict immigration and strict foreign-ownership rules tend to overlap), which is worth naming honestly. Yield chasing — buying where headline rental yields look higher than at home — is the motive most likely to ignore the cost stack this module covers next, because a 7% gross yield abroad can easily net out below a 4% yield at home once surcharges, financing penalties, and currency risk are all priced in.

四種動機構成了多數跨境住宅購買行為，每一種都值得誠實檢視，而不是套用行銷口號。分散風險——把曝險分散到單一母國房市週期、貨幣與政策風險之外——是真實存在的，但一間海外公寓其實是集中、缺乏流動性、完全沒有分散化的單一城市賭注，而不是一個分散化的部位；真正的分散化通常需要跨多個市場持有多筆物件（對個人而言很少實際可行），或是本課結尾談到的 REIT 替代方案。子女教育——提早幾年在目標大學或學區附近買房——是一項真實而常見的動機，但它是按單一家庭的時間表來決定住房，而不是按物件本身的投資價值，而且這類物件經常在教育需求結束的那一年就匆忙出售，不管當時市場條件如何。移民避險——在未來可能想搬去、或想送家人去的國家先卡一個立足點——確實有真實的選擇權價值，但這項價值最高的地方，往往正是合法購買最困難的地方（移民規則嚴格的國家，跟外國持有規則嚴格的國家高度重疊），這一點值得誠實點出。追逐殖利率——因為海外的名目租金殖利率看起來比國內高而買——是最容易忽略本課接下來要談的成本堆疊的動機，因為海外 7% 的毛殖利率，一旦把附加稅、融資懲罰與匯率風險都算進去，淨值很容易低於國內的 4% 殖利率。

The Regime Map: 34 Jurisdictions, One Spectrum

制度地圖：34 個司法轄區，一條光譜

This site's dashboard.html maintains a live foreign-buyer cost tracker across 34 jurisdictions, recording each market's extra tax percentage above what a local buyer pays, whether foreigners can buy at all, and the documented policy driving it. Laid end to end, the 34 jurisdictions span the entire spectrum from open to closed. At one extreme, Singapore's Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) charges foreign individual buyers 60% on top of the standard Buyer's Stamp Duty (since April 2023) — meaning a foreigner buying a S$2,000,000 condo pays over S$1,200,000 in stamp duty alone, before the property has generated a cent of return. At the other extreme, Canada's federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act (in force through at least 1 January 2027) bans most non-resident individuals from buying outright, and Australia bans foreign purchase of established (resale) dwellings entirely (new-build only, with approval), stacking a 9% NSW surcharge purchaser duty plus a 5% annual surcharge land tax on top of whatever narrow new-build purchases remain allowed. In between sits a wide middle: South Korea imposes no foreign-specific tax at all, but since August 2025 all of Seoul is a Land Transaction Permit Zone requiring any buyer — local or foreign — to move in within four months and reside two years, which functionally blocks pure investment purchases without ever naming foreigners in the statute. At the open end, the United States and Japan charge no foreign-buyer surcharge whatsoever, and the United Kingdom's non-resident surcharge is a comparatively modest flat 2% on top of standard, steeply progressive Stamp Duty Land Tax rates.

本站的 dashboard.html 維護一份涵蓋 34 個司法轄區的即時外國買方成本追蹤表，記錄每個市場相對於本地買方要多付的稅率百分比、外國人是否能買，以及背後有文獻記載的政策依據。把這 34 個司法轄區攤開排列，橫跨了從開放到封閉的整條光譜。光譜一端，新加坡的額外買方印花稅（ABSD）對外國個人買方課徵標準買方印花稅之外再加 60%（自 2023 年 4 月起）——意味著一位外國人買下一戶 200 萬新元的公寓，光是印花稅就要付超過 120 萬新元，物件連一分錢的報酬都還沒開始產生。光譜另一端，加拿大的《禁止非加拿大人購買住宅不動產法》（聯邦法規，至少施行到 2027 年 1 月 1 日）幾乎完全禁止非居民個人買房，而澳洲則完全禁止外國人購買既有（轉售）住宅（僅限新成屋、且須經核准），並在僅存的少數新成屋購買上，疊加 9% 的新南威爾斯州附加購屋印花稅，外加 5% 的年度附加地稅。中間則是一大片灰色地帶：南韓完全沒有針對外國人的專屬稅，但自 2025 年 8 月起，整個首爾都被劃為土地交易許可區，要求任何買方——不論本地人還是外國人——須在四個月內遷入、並居住滿兩年，這在法條中完全沒有點名外國人的情況下，實質上封鎖了純粹的投資性購買。而在開放這一端，美國與日本完全不對外國買方課徵附加稅，英國的非居民附加稅則相對溫和，是在標準、且高度累進的印花稅稅率之上，加徵固定 2% 的稅率。

🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

In July 2016, British Columbia announced a 15% Additional Property Transfer Tax on foreign buyers in Metro Vancouver — with roughly two weeks' notice before it took effect on August 2. Buyers already under contract but not yet closed found themselves owing a tax that did not exist when they signed; some scrambled to close before the deadline, others tried and failed to renegotiate price with sellers to absorb the shock. The tax was raised to 20% in 2018 and extended province-wide, and Vancouver house prices, which had been climbing double digits annually driven substantially by foreign capital inflows, cooled sharply within months of the announcement — a textbook demonstration that a foreign-buyer surcharge is not merely a cost line item but a policy tool capable of moving an entire market's price trajectory, deployed with almost no advance warning.

2016 年 7 月，英屬哥倫比亞省宣布對大溫哥華地區的外國買方課徵 15% 的額外財產移轉稅——距離 8 月 2 日生效，只給了大約兩週的預告期。已經簽約但尚未交割的買方，赫然發現自己欠下一筆簽約當時根本不存在的稅；有些人趕在期限前搶著交割，有些人試圖跟賣方重新協商價格以吸收這筆衝擊，但沒能成功。這項稅率在 2018 年調高到 20%，並擴大到全省適用，而溫哥華房價——原本受外國資金大量流入驅動，年增率高達兩位數——在政策宣布後幾個月內就急遽降溫。這是一堂教科書等級的示範：外國買方附加稅不只是一項成本項目，而是能在幾乎沒有任何預告的情況下部署、足以改變整個市場價格軌跡的政策工具。

The Full Cross-Border Cost Stack

完整的跨境成本堆疊

The surcharge tax in the table above is the most visible cross-border cost, but it is only one layer of a stack that a purely domestic buyer never encounters. Financing penalty — nonresident buyers are routinely offered a lower loan-to-value cap than a local buyer on the identical property (a 50-60% LTV ceiling for a foreign buyer where a local buyer might get 75-80%), and often a rate premium on top, because the lender is pricing both the buyer's weaker local credit history and its own weaker legal recourse against a borrower who may leave the country. Currency conversion spread — every cross-border wire transfer of purchase funds, ongoing mortgage payments, and eventual sale proceeds passes through a currency conversion that costs something (a bank's spread on the exchange rate, or a specialist FX broker's smaller but still real markup), a cost that compounds over the years of a hold and is easy to underestimate as "just the exchange rate." Ongoing dual filing obligations — a foreign owner typically owes tax filings in both the property's country (on rental income and eventual gain) and their own country of residence (which may tax worldwide income), and while tax treaties usually prevent the same dollar of income being taxed twice, they rarely eliminate the compliance burden of filing correctly in both places every single year, a cost in time and accounting fees that persists for as long as the property is held.

上表的附加稅是最顯眼的跨境成本，但它只是純國內買方永遠不會遇到的整套成本堆疊中的一層。融資懲罰——非居民買方在同一間物件上，經常只能拿到比本地買方更低的貸款成數上限（外國買方可能只有 50-60% 的貸款成數上限，本地買方可能拿得到 75-80%），且通常還要外加利率加碼，因為放款人同時在為買方較弱的當地信用記錄、以及自己對一位可能離境的借款人較弱的法律追索權定價。匯兌價差——每一筆跨境電匯，不論是購屋款項、後續的房貸還款，還是最終的出售價款，都要經過一次匯兌轉換，而這是有成本的（銀行的匯率價差，或專業外匯經紀商較小、但依然真實存在的加碼），這筆成本會在持有的多年間不斷累積，很容易被輕描淡寫成「不過就是匯率而已」。持續性的雙重申報義務——外國業主通常同時在物件所在國（就租金所得與最終利得）與自己的居住地國（可能對全球所得課稅）都負有申報義務，雖然租稅協定通常能避免同一筆所得被雙重課稅，但幾乎不會消除「每年都要在兩地正確申報」這項合規負擔，這是一項只要持有物件就會持續存在的時間與會計費用成本。

Worked Example — Currency Risk Can Erase a Winning Property (假設 Hypothetical)逐步演算範例——匯率風險能吃掉一筆賺錢的物件（假設數字）
  1. 1 假設 an investor with home currency HC buys a property abroad for the local-currency equivalent of HC 500,000 at an exchange rate of 1 HC = 1 local currency unit (LCU). Three years later, the property has appreciated 15% in local-currency terms — a genuinely strong result by local standards. 假設一位本國貨幣為 HC 的投資人，以匯率 1 HC = 1 當地貨幣單位（LCU）購入一筆海外物件，等值 HC 500,000。三年後，該物件以當地貨幣計算上漲了 15%——就當地標準而言，這是一項相當不錯的成績。
  2. 2 Local-currency value at sale: LCU 500,000 × 1.15 = LCU 575,000. In isolation, that looks like a clean 15% gain. 出售時的當地貨幣價值：LCU 500,000 × 1.15 = LCU 575,000。單獨來看，這是一筆乾淨的 15% 漲幅。
  3. 3 But over the same three years, 假設 the local currency depreciates 20% against the investor's home currency, so the exchange rate moves to 1 HC = 1.25 LCU. Converting the sale proceeds back: LCU 575,000 ÷ 1.25 = HC 460,000. 但在同樣的三年間，假設當地貨幣兌投資人本國貨幣貶值 20%，匯率變為 1 HC = 1.25 LCU。把出售價款換算回本國貨幣：LCU 575,000 ÷ 1.25 = HC 460,000。
  4. 4 In home-currency terms, the "winning" property actually lost value: HC 460,000 vs. HC 500,000 invested = −8% in home-currency terms, before even subtracting transaction costs, surcharge tax, or financing penalties. A property that outperformed its own local market can still be a losing investment once translated back to the currency the owner actually spends. 以本國貨幣計算，這筆「賺錢」的物件實際上是虧損的：HC 460,000 vs. 投入的 HC 500,000 ＝以本國貨幣計算為 −8%，這還沒扣掉交易成本、附加稅或融資懲罰。一筆在當地市場表現優於大盤的物件，換算回業主實際花用的貨幣之後，依然可能是一筆虧損的投資。
  5. 5 The most direct hedge against this specific risk is financing the purchase with a local-currency mortgage rather than home-currency cash: if the debt is denominated in the same currency that depreciated, the loan balance shrinks in home-currency terms right alongside the asset — the currency mismatch that hurt an all-cash buyer largely cancels out for a buyer who borrowed locally, though it introduces its own local interest-rate risk in exchange. 針對這項特定風險，最直接的避險方式，是用當地貨幣房貸融資購買，而不是用本國貨幣現金全額支付：如果債務是以貶值的那個貨幣計價，貸款餘額換算回本國貨幣時也會同步縮水——這正好抵銷了讓全額現金買方受傷的貨幣錯配，只不過交換條件是背上了當地利率的風險。

The Distance-Management Problem

遠距管理問題

A property an owner cannot easily visit creates a management problem that has nothing to do with tax or currency. Agent dependence is close to total — a distant owner has no practical way to verify a local property manager's claims about occupancy, maintenance, or repair costs beyond the numbers the manager chooses to report, and no easy way to switch managers without a costly, disruptive search conducted from another time zone. The empty-unit deterioration spiral is a documented pattern in cross-border markets: a vacant unit with no local eyes on it goes unnoticed longer, minor issues (a slow leak, a failed appliance, pest entry) compound into major ones before anyone local reports them, and by the time the distant owner learns of the problem the repair bill is far larger than an early catch would have produced. Scam exposure is the sharpest version of the problem: guaranteed-rental-return schemes — a developer or agent promising a fixed rental yield for the first few years of ownership, common in overseas pre-sale marketing — have a well-documented pattern of the guarantee simply evaporating once the promotional period ends or the promising entity becomes insolvent, leaving the buyer holding a property whose real achievable rent was never anywhere near what was marketed.

一間業主無法輕易親自前往查看的物件，會產生一個跟稅務或匯率完全無關的管理問題。對代理人的依賴幾近全面——遠距業主幾乎沒有實際辦法核實當地物業經理關於出租率、維護或維修成本的說法，只能接受經理人選擇回報的數字，也很難在不透過一場從另一個時區進行、成本高昂又擾人的搜尋之下更換經理人。空置單位的惡化螺旋是跨境市場中一種文獻記載的模式：一個沒有當地人盯著的空置單位，問題會拖更久才被發現，小問題（緩慢的滲漏、故障的家電、蟲害入侵）會在有當地人回報之前，複合成大問題，等到遠距業主得知問題時，維修帳單往往遠大於及早發現時的成本。詐騙曝險是這個問題最尖銳的版本：保證租金報酬方案——開發商或仲介承諾持有前幾年有固定租金殖利率，在海外預售行銷中相當常見——有文獻記載清楚的模式，就是一旦促銷期結束、或承諾的實體本身破產，這項保證就會直接消失，留下業主手上一間實際可達租金從未接近行銷數字的物件。

Legal-System Risk: The Title-Security Spectrum

法律制度風險：產權安全光譜

Not every "title" means the same thing. At one end of the spectrum sit Torrens-style registriesA land registration system (originating in 19th-century Australia, now used across Australia, NZ, much of Canada, Singapore, and parts of the US and UK) where the government's register itself is the definitive proof of ownership, state-guaranteed against most competing claims — as opposed to relying on an unbroken chain of historical deeds.一種土地登記制度（起源於 19 世紀澳洲，現行於澳洲、紐西蘭、加拿大多數地區、新加坡，以及美英部分地區），政府的登記本身就是產權的決定性證明，由國家對多數競爭性主張提供擔保——而不是依賴一連串未曾中斷的歷史地契。, where the government register itself is the legally definitive proof of ownership, state-guaranteed against most competing claims — buying property here means the registry entry is close to bulletproof. At the other end sit weak-registry markets where title is proven through a chain of historical documents that can be forged, incomplete, or genuinely disputed by a competing claimant, and where a buyer's practical protection depends heavily on the quality of title insurance or local legal diligence rather than the registry itself. The most dangerous variant sits outside the leasehold framework entirely: a nominee structureAn arrangement where property or land is registered in a local citizen's name (often for a fee) while a foreign buyer who cannot legally hold it directly retains beneficial control through a private side agreement — illegal in the jurisdictions that restrict foreign land ownership specifically to prevent this workaround.一種安排：不動產或土地登記在一位本地公民名下（通常收取費用），而真正無法合法直接持有的外國買方，透過私下的附屬協議保留實質控制權——在那些正是為了防堵這種規避手法而限制外國持有土地的司法轄區，這種安排屬於違法行為。, where a local citizen's name goes on the title while a foreign buyer holds beneficial control through a private side agreement, specifically to work around a land-ownership ban. This is flagged hard, in both directions: it is illegal in most jurisdictions that restrict foreign land ownership precisely because they anticipate and prohibit this workaround, exposing the foreign buyer to prosecution and the nominee's own liability risk; and it is a genuine confiscation risk even where enforcement is lax, because the buyer's "ownership" has no standing in the country's actual property law — a nominee who dies, divorces, goes bankrupt, or simply decides to keep the property leaves the real foreign buyer with a private contract to enforce against someone else's legal title, in a court system that may not recognize the side agreement at all.

🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

Spain's Ley de Costas (Coastal Law), reinterpreted and enforced more aggressively from the 2000s onward, asserted that a strip of Spain's coastline is public maritime domain and cannot legally be privately owned — including, in a well-documented number of cases, land under homes that had been built and sold decades earlier, many to British and other foreign retirees who had bought in good faith with clean paperwork at the time. Owners in the affected zones found their properties reclassified: some faced eventual demolition orders, others were downgraded to a decaying 30-year concession that could not be renewed, sold, or passed to heirs on the same terms, destroying resale value years before the concession actually expired. The episode became one of the most widely cited cautionary tales in British overseas-property circles precisely because the buyers had done nothing wrong by the rules in force when they bought — the risk was that a foreign government's own domestic law changed retroactively in its practical effect, a risk no amount of individual buyer diligence could have priced in at the time of purchase.

西班牙的《海岸法》（Ley de Costas），自 2000 年代起被重新解釋並更積極地執法，主張西班牙海岸線的一段土地屬於公有海事領域，不能合法私有——文獻記載的案例中，這甚至包括數十年前就已興建並出售的住宅所在的土地，其中許多買家是當年憑善意、以當時乾淨合法的文件購入的英國及其他外國退休人士。受影響區域內的業主，眼看著自己的物件被重新分類：有些最終面臨拆除令，有些則被降級為一份逐漸凋零、不能續約、不能出售、也不能以相同條件傳給繼承人的 30 年特許權，在特許權真正到期前的好幾年，就先把轉售價值摧毀殆盡。這起事件之所以成為英國海外置產圈裡最常被引用的警世案例之一，正是因為買家依照自己購買當時有效的規則，完全沒有做錯任何事——風險在於，外國政府自己的國內法在實際效果上溯及既往地改變了，而這種風險，是任何個別買方的盡職調查，在購買當下都無法定價進去的。

🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

Thai law bars most foreigners from owning land directly, and Thai authorities have documented, since 2023-2024, an active crackdown on the nominee shareholder structures widely used to work around that restriction — typically a Thai company where a foreign buyer holds the economic interest through side agreements while Thai nominee shareholders hold the legal shares on paper, letting the "Thai company" buy land the foreign buyer's own name could never hold. Investigations targeted popular resort areas including Phuket, examining company registrations for the hallmark signs of nominee arrangements (Thai shareholders with no real capital contribution, no real role in the business, and share proportions that exist purely to clear the legal foreign-ownership threshold). The crackdown left buyers who had used these structures — often set up years earlier by a local lawyer or agent who presented it as standard practice — facing genuine legal exposure over land they believed, in practical terms, they already owned.

泰國法律禁止多數外國人直接持有土地，泰國當局自 2023 至 2024 年起，已文獻記載對廣泛用來規避這項限制的代持股東結構展開積極打擊——典型做法是設立一家泰國公司，外國買方透過私下協議持有經濟利益，而泰國代持股東則在紙面上持有法律股權，讓這家「泰國公司」得以購買外國買方自己名下永遠無法持有的土地。調查鎖定了包括普吉島在內的熱門度假地區，檢視公司登記中代持安排的典型特徵（泰國股東沒有真實出資、在公司中沒有實質角色、股權比例純粹是為了湊足法定外國持股門檻）。這波打擊行動，讓那些曾使用這類架構——往往是數年前由當地律師或仲介以「業界慣例」之名協助設立——的買家，就他們原以為在實務上早已擁有的土地，面臨真實的法律風險。

Tax-Treaty Basics and the Estate-Tax Trap

租稅協定基礎與遺產稅陷阱

A bilateral tax treaty between two countries typically does two things relevant to a cross-border property owner: it reduces the withholding tax rate applied to rent, interest, or sale proceeds sent across the border (often significantly below the statutory rate), and it establishes a mechanism — a foreign tax credit, an exemption, or an allocation rule — so the same income isn't fully taxed twice. Neither benefit is automatic; both usually require an active filing or certificate to claim, and doing nothing typically defaults to the higher, unrelieved rate.

兩國之間的雙邊租稅協定，通常對跨境物件業主做兩件相關的事：降低跨境匯出的租金、利息或出售價款所適用的扣繳稅率（經常明顯低於法定稅率），並建立一套機制——外國稅額扣抵、豁免，或分配規則——確保同一筆所得不會被完整課稅兩次。這兩項好處都不是自動生效的；通常都需要主動申報或取得證明才能申請，什麼都不做，通常預設會適用較高、未經減免的稅率。

One trap catches nonresident owners who never think of themselves as needing estate planning in a country they don't live in: many jurisdictions apply an estate or inheritance tax to situs assetsTax charged on assets physically or legally located within a country's borders when the owner dies, applied regardless of the owner's own residency or citizenship — meaning a foreign owner's home-country estate plan often does not cover, or even anticipate, this liability.業主過世時，對位於一國境內、具有法律上「所在地」屬性的資產課徵的稅，不論業主本身的居住地或國籍為何——這意味著外國業主母國的遺產規劃，往往完全沒有涵蓋、甚至根本沒有預期到這項稅負。 — property physically located within their borders — regardless of the owner's own residency or citizenship. The United States is the most documented archetype: a nonresident, non-citizen individual owning US real estate directly is subject to US estate tax on that property at death, but with an exemption threshold dramatically smaller than the one available to US citizens and residents (in the tens of thousands of dollars rather than the multi-million-dollar threshold citizens receive), meaning a foreign owner's estate can face a US tax bill their home-country estate plan never anticipated and their home-country will may not even address. This is precisely the kind of structuring question — direct ownership vs. a holding entity, insurance to cover the exposure — that belongs in front of a cross-border estate specialist well before it becomes urgent.

有一個陷阱，專門逮到那些從不覺得自己需要在一個自己不住的國家做遺產規劃的非居民業主：許多司法轄區會針對「所在地資產」課徵遺產稅或繼承稅Tax charged on assets physically or legally located within a country's borders when the owner dies, applied regardless of the owner's own residency or citizenship — meaning a foreign owner's home-country estate plan often does not cover, or even anticipate, this liability.業主過世時，對位於一國境內、具有法律上「所在地」屬性的資產課徵的稅，不論業主本身的居住地或國籍為何——這意味著外國業主母國的遺產規劃，往往完全沒有涵蓋、甚至根本沒有預期到這項稅負。——物理上位於其境內的物件——不論業主自己的居住地或國籍為何。美國是文獻記載最完整的典型：一位非居民、非公民的個人，若直接持有美國不動產，過世時該物件須適用美國遺產稅，但豁免門檻遠低於美國公民與居民能適用的門檻（僅數萬美元，而不是公民能拿到的數百萬美元門檻），意味著外國業主的遺產，可能面臨一筆母國遺產規劃從未預期到、甚至母國遺囑根本沒有處理到的美國稅單。這正是該在情況變得急迫之前，就交給跨境遺產規劃專家處理的架構問題——直接持有 vs. 透過持股實體持有、用保險來覆蓋這項曝險。

Exit Friction Abroad

海外出場摩擦

Selling and getting the money home carries its own friction that a domestic sale never does. Some countries impose capital controls — formal limits on how much currency can be converted and repatriated per year, or a requirement to route the transaction through central-bank-approved channels — that can delay or cap how much of a sale's proceeds actually make it back to the owner's home currency in the year of sale. Even without formal controls, repatriation requires navigating the destination country's own reporting requirements for large incoming international transfers, which can trigger source-of-funds documentation demands that a domestic sale never generates. And selling-cost norms vary far more across borders than most first-time cross-border sellers expect: agent commissions, mandatory notary or lawyer involvement, and closing-cost splits between buyer and seller follow local convention, not the seller's home-market intuition, and can add several extra percentage points of cost that a seller who only ever sold domestically simply didn't budget for.

出售並把錢匯回家，帶有國內交易永遠不會有的摩擦。有些國家會實施資本管制——正式限制每年能兌換與匯出多少貨幣，或要求交易須透過央行核准的管道進行——這可能延遲、或限制一筆出售價款在出售當年，實際能匯回業主本國貨幣的金額。即使沒有正式的管制，資金匯回也需要應付目的地國家自己對大額跨境匯入款的申報要求，這可能觸發國內交易從不會產生的資金來源文件要求。而出售成本慣例跨境差異之大，遠超過多數首次跨境出售者的預期：仲介佣金、強制公證人或律師的參與、買賣雙方之間交割成本的分攤，都依循當地慣例，而不是賣方本國市場的直覺，可能額外增加好幾個百分點的成本，是一位只在國內賣過房的賣方，根本沒有編列預算的支出。

The Honest Alternative: Global REITs

誠實的替代方案：全球型 REIT

Everything above — surcharges, financing penalties, currency risk, distance management, title-security spectrum, estate traps, exit friction — is the price of getting direct, physical, single-property exposure to a foreign housing market. A globally diversified REIT (covered in depth in Module 11) buys the same underlying economic exposure — real estate income and appreciation, spread across many properties and often many countries — without a single one of those frictions: no foreign-buyer surcharge, no nonresident financing penalty, instant liquidity instead of a slow cross-border sale, no nominee risk, no estate-tax situs trap on a single physical asset, and genuine diversification across dozens or hundreds of properties instead of a concentrated bet on one city. The tradeoff runs the other way too — no control over the specific asset, no ability to physically use the property, and REIT share prices carry their own volatility and correlate more with equity markets than a single quiet rental typically does. For a buyer whose real motive is diversification or yield rather than a specific personal-use case (the education or migration motives from this module's opening), the REIT alternative deserves a serious side-by-side comparison before signing anything abroad — not an afterthought.
