Pre-Sales & New Builds

預售與新成屋

What You'll Learn這一課學什麼
  • How a presale payment schedule actually works, and the deposit-leverage mechanics that can multiply a gain — or turn a modest price drop into a loss larger than the money put down.一份預售付款排程實際上如何運作，以及能夠放大獲利——或把小幅價格下跌變成超過已付訂金的虧損——的訂金槓桿機制。
  • Presale-specific risks in depth: developer solvency (using the 2021–24 China unfinished-building crisis as the canonical documented case), completion-risk protections that vary sharply by jurisdiction, specification risk, and area-shrinkage surprises.深入了解預售特有的風險：建商信用（以 2021 至 2024 年中國爛尾樓危機作為典型有紀錄可循的案例）、依司法轄區差異極大的完工風險保護機制、規格風險，以及坪數縮水的意外狀況。
  • How to evaluate a developer before signing, and the new-build-versus-resale economics that decide whether the new-build premium is actually worth paying.簽約前如何評估一家建商，以及決定新成屋溢價是否真正值得付出的新成屋對比中古屋經濟學。
  • Assignment/sub-sale flipping mechanics where it's legal, and the regulatory crackdowns that have hit it in multiple jurisdictions.在合法的司法轄區裡，轉約／預售屋轉售的操作機制，以及多個司法轄區已經祭出的監管打壓。
  • Completion-stage discipline: inspecting before accepting, and timing your mortgage drawdown correctly against your rate lock and any existing lease.完工階段的紀律：驗屋在點交之前完成，以及把貸款撥款時機正確地對齊你的利率鎖定與任何既有租約。

A presale contract is, underneath the marketing brochures and the show flat, a forward contract on an asset that doesn't exist yet, financed with a fraction of its price paid up front. That structure is exactly what makes presale attractive — a buyer can control a future asset for 10–15% down instead of the 20–30% a completed property typically demands — and exactly what makes it dangerous, because every risk that would normally be visible in a finished building (does it actually get built, does it match what was shown, is the developer still solvent by completion) is instead a forecast made years in advance on a set of blueprints. This module works through the mechanics, the risks, the evaluation discipline, and the completion-stage decisions a presale buyer has to get right, because unlike a resale purchase, a bad presale decision often can't be discovered until it's too late to walk away.

一份預售契約，在行銷手冊與樣品屋之下，其實是一份針對一個尚不存在的資產所簽訂的遠期合約，只用一小部分價格提前支付來融資。這個結構正是讓預售有吸引力的原因——買方能用 10% 到 15% 的訂金掌控一個未來資產，而不是一間完工物件通常要求的 20% 到 30%——也正是讓它危險的原因，因為每一項通常在完工建築物裡就能看得見的風險（到底會不會真的蓋出來、會不會跟展示的一樣、建商到完工前是否還有償債能力），都變成了根據一套藍圖提前好幾年做出的預測。這一課會逐一拆解機制、風險、評估紀律，以及預售買方在完工階段必須做對的決策，因為不同於中古屋購買，一個糟糕的預售決策，往往要等到為時已晚、已經無法脫身時才會被發現。

1. How presale actually works: deposit, staged payments, completion

一、預售實際上如何運作：訂金、期付款、完工交屋

A presale purchase moves through a payment structure fundamentally different from a resale closing, where the full price (minus a mortgage) changes hands in a single transaction. Presale spreads payment across the construction timeline, with each stage paymentA partial payment due at a specific construction milestone (foundation, structure topped out, interior fit-out, etc.), as opposed to a lump-sum payment at closing. The share of price collected before construction even begins, and how payments are spread across milestones after that, varies enormously by developer and jurisdiction.在特定營建里程碑（打地基、結構封頂、室內裝修等）到期應付的部分款項，有別於在成交時一次付清。動工前就收取的價格比例、以及之後款項如何依里程碑分攤，因建商與司法轄區而有極大差異。 tied to a construction milestone rather than a calendar date, which is itself an important distinction: a milestone-tied schedule slows down automatically if construction slows down, while a calendar-tied schedule keeps demanding payment on schedule even if the building is falling behind, shifting timing risk onto the buyer.

預售購買會經過一套與中古屋成交結構根本不同的付款流程；在中古屋成交中，全額價金（扣除房貸部分）在單一筆交易中一次易手。預售則把付款分攤在整個營建時程上，每一筆期付款A partial payment due at a specific construction milestone (foundation, structure topped out, interior fit-out, etc.), as opposed to a lump-sum payment at closing. The share of price collected before construction even begins, and how payments are spread across milestones after that, varies enormously by developer and jurisdiction.在特定營建里程碑（打地基、結構封頂、室內裝修等）到期應付的部分款項，有別於在成交時一次付清。動工前就收取的價格比例、以及之後款項如何依里程碑分攤，因建商與司法轄區而有極大差異。都與一個營建里程碑而非日曆日期綁定，這本身就是一個重要的區別：一套與里程碑綁定的排程，會在營建進度放慢時自動跟著放慢；而一套與日曆綁定的排程，即使建築進度落後，仍會依原訂時程要求付款，把時程風險轉嫁給買方。

A Typical Presale Payment Schedule (Illustrative Shape, Varies by Jurisdiction)典型的預售付款排程（示意結構，依司法轄區而異）
  1. 1 Reservation deposit (at contract signing): typically 1–5% of price, often the least refundable portion if the buyer walks away. 預訂訂金（簽約時）：通常為價格的1% 至 5%，若買方毀約，往往是最不可退還的一部分。
  2. 2 Balance of down payment (within weeks of signing, before construction starts in earnest): brings the total upfront commitment to roughly 10–15% in many markets, though this figure ranges from single digits to 30%+ depending on jurisdiction and developer. 訂金餘款（簽約後數週內，通常在營建正式展開之前）：把前期總承諾拉高到許多市場中大約10% 至 15%，但這個數字依司法轄區與建商不同，範圍從個位數到 30% 以上都有。
  3. 3 Construction-milestone stage payments (foundation complete, structure topped out, exterior enclosed, interior fit-out): each typically 5–15% of price, collectively bringing the total paid before completion to somewhere well short of the full price — the remainder is financed at closing. 營建里程碑期付款（地基完工、結構封頂、外殼封閉、室內裝修）：每一期通常為價格的5% 至 15%，累計起來，在完工前已付總額會明顯低於全額價格——剩餘部分在交屋時透過貸款融資支付。
  4. 4 Completion/handover payment: the balance of the contract price, typically financed with a mortgage that only becomes available once the unit is complete and titled — which is why a presale buyer's mortgage pre-approval, done years earlier, has to be re-confirmed (and can fail to re-qualify) at completion. 完工／交屋款：契約價金的餘額，通常透過房貸融資支付，而這筆貸款要等到單位完工並取得產權後才會核撥——這也是為什麼預售買方好幾年前就做好的房貸初核，到了交屋時必須重新確認（而且有可能無法通過複核）。

Leverage on the deposit: controlling a future asset with a fraction of its price

訂金上的槓桿：用一小部分價格掌控一個未來資產

The arithmetic that makes presale attractive to speculators, and dangerous to anyone who underestimates it, is simple leverage: putting down 10–15% to control an asset means any price move between contract and completion is applied to the full contract price, not to the fraction actually paid, which multiplies both gains and losses relative to the cash committed.

讓預售對投機者有吸引力、也讓低估它的人陷入危險的算術，其實就是簡單的槓桿：付出 10% 到 15% 就能掌控一個資產，代表簽約到完工之間任何價格波動，都是套用在全額契約價格上，而不是實際已付的那一小部分，這讓獲利與虧損相對於已投入的現金都被放大。

Worked Example — Deposit Leverage, Both Directions (假設 Hypothetical)逐步演算範例——訂金槓桿，正反兩種方向（假設案例）
  1. 1 Contract price at presale signing: $600,000. Total cash paid through completion (down payment + stage payments): 12% = $72,000. 預售簽約時的契約價格：$600,000。到完工為止已付現金總額（訂金＋期付款）：12%＝$72,000。
  2. 2 Upside case: market value at completion is +20% above contract price = $720,000. Paper gain = $120,000 against $72,000 of cash actually paid in → a 167% return on the cash committed — roughly 8× the raw 20% market move, because the gain applies to the full $600,000 base, not the $72,000 the buyer actually put at risk. 上漲情境：完工時市值比契約價格高出+20%＝$720,000。帳面獲利＝$120,000，對比實際投入的 $72,000 現金 → 相當於投入現金報酬率 167%——大約是原始 20% 市場漲幅的 8 倍，因為獲利是套用在全額 $600,000 的基礎上，而不是買方實際承擔風險的 $72,000。
  3. 3 Downside case: market value at completion is −15% below contract price = $510,000. The buyer is still contractually obligated to complete at the original $600,000 price — either by closing anyway (financing $600,000 against a $510,000 asset, an immediate $90,000 paper loss against $72,000 paid in, i.e. the loss exceeds the entire deposit) or by defaulting, which in most jurisdictions forfeits some or all of the deposit already paid and may expose the buyer to further claims for the developer's resale shortfall, depending on the contract and jurisdiction. 下跌情境：完工時市值比契約價格低−15%＝$510,000。買方依契約仍有義務以原本 $600,000 的價格完成交易——要嘛照樣成交（用 $600,000 融資買下一個只值 $510,000 的資產，立即產生 $90,000 的帳面虧損，超過已付入的 $72,000，也就是虧損超過整筆訂金），要嘛違約，而在多數司法轄區違約會沒收部分或全部已付訂金，且依契約與司法轄區不同，買方可能還要為建商轉售短少的差額承擔進一步求償責任。
  4. 4 Break-even for the deposit specifically: the market move at which loss exactly equals the deposit paid in is −12% (the deposit percentage itself) — any decline worse than that, and the buyer's exposure exceeds 100% of cash committed even before counting a potential deficiency claim. 訂金本身的損益兩平點：虧損恰好等於已付訂金的市場跌幅是−12%（也就是訂金比例本身）——任何比這更糟的跌幅，買方的曝險就會超過已投入現金的 100%，這還不包括可能的差額求償。
⚠️
Pitfall:常見陷阱： Thinking of a presale deposit the way you'd think of a resale down payment — as the most you can lose. In most jurisdictions a presale contract is a binding obligation to complete at the contracted price, not an option you can simply walk away from at the cost of the deposit; a large enough price decline can expose the buyer to losses beyond 100% of cash paid in. 把預售訂金當成中古屋頭期款來看待——以為那是你最多會虧損的金額。在多數司法轄區，預售契約是一份必須以契約價格完成交易的約束性義務，不是一個你可以只損失訂金就隨意棄約的選擇權；跌幅夠大時，買方的虧損可能超過已付入現金的 100%。

2. Presale-specific risks in depth

二、深入了解預售特有的風險

Developer solvency and completion-risk protections

建商信用與完工風險保護機制

The single largest presale risk is that the building never gets finished at all, because the developer runs out of money, over-leverages against a portfolio of projects rather than just the one being sold, or diverts buyer deposits to fund construction on a different, unrelated project. Whether a buyer is meaningfully protected against this risk depends almost entirely on jurisdiction, and the protections range from strong to nearly nonexistent. Escrow/trust-account regimesA legal requirement that buyer deposits and stage payments be held in a segregated, regulated account and released to the developer only against verified construction progress, rather than flowing directly into the developer's general operating funds. Strong versions of this regime meaningfully limit a developer's ability to divert buyer money to unrelated projects or general corporate expenses.法律要求買方訂金與期付款必須存放在一個獨立、受監管的帳戶裡，只有在核實營建進度後才能撥付給建商，而不是直接流入建商的一般營運資金。這種制度的強版本能有意義地限制建商把買方資金挪用到無關專案或一般公司開支的能力。 require deposits to sit in a segregated, regulated account released only against verified construction progress, sharply limiting a developer's ability to divert buyer money elsewhere. Jurisdiction-flagged（依司法轄區而異）: some markets mandate this by law with real enforcement; others have a nominally similar rule with weak enforcement; others have essentially no protection at all, meaning buyer deposits become an unsecured claim against the developer's general assets if it fails — a buyer must verify which regime actually applies before assuming any protection exists.

單一最大的預售風險，是建築物根本從未完工，原因可能是建商資金耗盡、把槓桿過度壓在一整批專案而非單一在售專案上，或是把買方訂金挪用去資助另一個無關專案的營建。買方是否真的受到這個風險的有意義保護，幾乎完全取決於司法轄區，保護程度從強到近乎不存在都有。履約保證／信託帳戶制度A legal requirement that buyer deposits and stage payments be held in a segregated, regulated account and released to the developer only against verified construction progress, rather than flowing directly into the developer's general operating funds. Strong versions of this regime meaningfully limit a developer's ability to divert buyer money to unrelated projects or general corporate expenses.法律要求買方訂金與期付款必須存放在一個獨立、受監管的帳戶裡，只有在核實營建進度後才能撥付給建商，而不是直接流入建商的一般營運資金。這種制度的強版本能有意義地限制建商把買方資金挪用到無關專案或一般公司開支的能力。要求訂金存放在一個獨立、受監管的帳戶裡，只有核實營建進度後才能撥付，大幅限制建商把買方資金挪用到別處的能力。依司法轄區而異：有些市場以法律強制規定並確實執法；有些有名義上類似的規則但執法薄弱；有些則幾乎沒有任何保護，意味著一旦建商倒閉，買方訂金就變成對建商一般資產的無擔保債權——買方必須先確認自己所在的制度到底屬於哪一種，而不是預設任何保護都存在。

⚠️
Pitfall:常見陷阱： Assuming "my deposit is in escrow" without verifying who actually controls the account, what triggers a release, and whether the regime has real regulatory teeth. A nominal escrow requirement with weak oversight can function little better than no protection at all — jurisdiction-flagged, verify the specific legal regime before trusting the label. 在沒有查核帳戶到底由誰控管、什麼條件觸發撥款、以及這套制度是否有真正監管牙齒的情況下，就假設「我的訂金有履約保證」。一個名義上有履約保證要求、但監管薄弱的制度，實際保護效果可能跟完全沒有保護差不了多少——依司法轄區而異，在信任這個標籤之前，先查核具體的法律制度。

Specification risk, area shrinkage, and completion slippage

規格風險、坪數縮水與完工延遲

Even when a project completes and the developer stays solvent, three narrower risks routinely surprise presale buyers. Specification risk is the gap between what the show flat displays and what actually gets delivered — a fully furnished, professionally staged, often larger-than-actual show unit sets an anchor expectation that the delivered unit, built to the contract's actual spec sheet (not the display's), routinely falls short of; the discipline here is reading the spec sheet line by line rather than trusting the visual impression of the display suite, and confirming which finishes are contractually guaranteed versus merely "similar to" what's shown. Area shrinkage, and specifically surprises in 公設比The share of a unit's stated (and often priced) floor area that is actually common/shared space — lobbies, corridors, mechanical rooms, sometimes even structural walls — rather than the buyer's own usable interior. A high or opaque common-area ratio means a buyer is paying full price per square foot for space they can never actually occupy exclusively.一戶單位登記（且通常據以計價）的坪數中，實際上屬於公共／共用空間（大廳、走廊、機電室，有時甚至包含結構牆）而非買方自己可獨佔使用室內空間的比例。過高或不透明的公設比，代表買方是用全額每坪單價，付錢買下一塊自己永遠無法真正獨佔使用的空間。 (the common-area ratio embedded in the priced floor area), can mean a buyer is paying full per-square-foot price for lobby, corridor, and mechanical-room space that contributes nothing to livable interior area — a risk that's jurisdiction-flagged, since disclosure requirements and typical common-area ratios vary widely by market. Completion-date slippage hits a buyer's finances directly through two channels that don't show up on the purchase contract itself: a mortgage rate lock secured months or years earlier can expire before a delayed completion, forcing a re-lock at a worse rate, and a buyer who timed an existing lease to end at the original completion date can be left needing bridge housing, at their own cost, for however long the delay runs.

即使一個專案順利完工、建商也維持償債能力，三個較狹窄的風險仍然經常讓預售買方大吃一驚。規格風險是樣品屋展示的內容與實際交付內容之間的落差——一間全套裝潢、經過專業佈置、往往比實際格局更大的樣品屋，會設定一個錨定期望，而依契約實際規格表（而非展示屋規格）交付的單位，經常達不到這個期望；這裡的紀律是逐行讀規格表，而不是相信展示套房的視覺印象，並確認哪些裝修是契約保證的，哪些只是「與展示相似」。坪數縮水，尤其是公設比The share of a unit's stated (and often priced) floor area that is actually common/shared space — lobbies, corridors, mechanical rooms, sometimes even structural walls — rather than the buyer's own usable interior. A high or opaque common-area ratio means a buyer is paying full price per square foot for space they can never actually occupy exclusively.一戶單位登記（且通常據以計價）的坪數中，實際上屬於公共／共用空間（大廳、走廊、機電室，有時甚至包含結構牆）而非買方自己可獨佔使用室內空間的比例。過高或不透明的公設比，代表買方是用全額每坪單價，付錢買下一塊自己永遠無法真正獨佔使用的空間。（登記計價坪數中內含的公共空間比例）上的意外，可能代表買方正用全額每坪單價，為完全無助於可居住室內面積的大廳、走廊與機電室空間付錢——這是一項依司法轄區而異的風險，因為揭露要求與典型公設比因市場而有極大差異。完工延遲透過兩個不會出現在買賣契約本身的管道，直接打擊買方的財務：好幾個月甚至好幾年前鎖定的房貸利率，可能在延遲後的完工日之前就已到期，迫使買方以更差的利率重新鎖定；而一位把既有租約時間安排在原訂完工日結束的買方，可能會發現自己需要自費、且不知道要多久的過渡性住房。

🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

China's 2021–2024 unfinished-building (爛尾樓) crisis is the canonical, extensively documented case of developer-solvency risk materializing at national scale. Evergrande, one of China's largest developers, defaulted on offshore debt in late 2021 after years of aggressive leverage across a vast portfolio of presold projects; Country Garden and a wave of other major developers followed with their own defaults and construction halts through 2022–2023. Because a large share of Chinese presale buyers had paid substantial deposits and stage payments — often the majority of a household's life savings — into projects that then stopped construction entirely, hundreds of thousands of buyers across dozens of cities were left, according to widely reported industry estimates, owning mortgage obligations on units that might never be completed, in a market where the escrow/pre-sale-fund-supervision regime intended to prevent exactly this outcome had, in many documented instances, been circumvented or under-enforced. The episode triggered a wave of buyer mortgage boycotts in 2022 that was itself widely covered internationally, and prompted subsequent regulatory reforms in China tightening pre-sale fund supervision — reforms that arrived only after buyers who had already committed capital had no recourse to undo the decision.

中國 2021 至 2024 年的爛尾樓危機，是建商信用風險在全國規模上實現的典型、有充分紀錄可循的案例。恒大是中國最大的建商之一，在對一大批預售專案採取多年激進槓桿操作後，於 2021 年底對境外債務違約；碧桂園與一波其他主要建商在 2022 至 2023 年間陸續跟進，各自出現違約與停工。因為相當一部分中國預售買方已經把可觀的訂金與期付款——往往是一個家庭畢生積蓄的大部分——投入了後來完全停工的專案，根據廣泛報導的業界估計，在數十個城市裡，數十萬名買方最終背負著可能永遠不會完工的單位的房貸義務，而在這個市場裡，原本用來防止這種結果發生的履約保證／預售資金監管制度，在許多有紀錄的案例中，已被規避或執法不足。這起事件在 2022 年引發了一波買方集體斷供房貸的行動，這波行動本身也獲得國際媒體廣泛報導，並促使中國隨後推動監管改革、收緊預售資金監管——但這些改革，是在已經投入資金的買方已無法挽回其決定之後才姍姍來遲。

3. Evaluating a developer before signing

三、簽約前評估一家建商

Given how much of presale risk traces back to a single counterparty, developer due diligence deserves at least as much attention as the unit itself. Four checks matter most. Track record: has this specific developer delivered previous projects on time, at the promised specification, without a history of buyer disputes or litigation over delays and defects — a developer's own past projects, visited and inspected years after handover, are a far more reliable signal than its current marketing materials. Financial leverage signals: how many projects is the developer running simultaneously relative to its balance sheet, how dependent is it on presale deposits themselves to fund ongoing construction (a developer using buyer deposits as working capital rather than as a supplement to its own equity and construction financing is structurally fragile to any slowdown in sales), and what does its public debt, if any, signal about market-perceived credit risk. Presold-inventory dependence: a project that's 95% presold before breaking ground is in a fundamentally different risk position than one still 30% unsold at the same stage, because the latter's construction financing may depend on sales momentum that hasn't materialized, giving the developer a live incentive to cut corners or delay disclosure of problems to keep sales alive. The too-good-discount flag: a presale offering priced meaningfully below comparable nearby projects is occasionally a genuine early-mover discount, but it is also one of the most common signals of a developer under cash-flow pressure trying to accelerate sales velocity at any cost — the discount itself is information, and it should trigger deeper diligence rather than being read as pure upside.

既然預售風險有這麼大一部分可以追溯回單一一個交易對手，建商盡職調查值得至少跟單位本身一樣多的關注。四項檢查最為重要。過往紀錄：這家特定建商過去的專案是否準時交屋、規格符合承諾、沒有因延遲或瑕疵而與買方發生糾紛或訴訟的紀錄——親自造訪並檢視建商過去在交屋數年後的專案，遠比它目前的行銷資料更可靠。財務槓桿訊號：相對於其資產負債表，這家建商同時在進行多少個專案、它對用預售訂金本身來資助持續營建的依賴程度有多深（一家把買方訂金當成營運資金、而非自有股本與營建融資補充的建商，對任何銷售放緩都結構性脆弱），以及它的公開負債（如果有的話）對市場認知的信用風險傳達了什麼訊息。預售存量依賴度：一個在動工前就已預售 95% 的專案，跟一個在同一階段仍有 30% 未售出的專案，處在根本不同的風險位置，因為後者的營建融資可能依賴尚未實現的銷售動能，這給了建商一個現實的誘因，去偷工減料或延後揭露問題，以維持銷售動能。「好到不像話」折扣警訊：一個預售案定價明顯低於附近可比專案，偶爾確實是真正的早鳥折扣，但這也是建商正承受現金流壓力、不計代價想加速銷售速度的最常見訊號之一——折扣本身就是一項資訊，應該觸發更深入的盡職調查，而不是被解讀為純粹的上檔空間。

5. Assignment / 轉約 flipping

6. Completion-stage decisions: inspect before you accept

六、完工階段的決策：驗屋在點交之前

The final stretch of a presale purchase — from completion notice to key handover — is where months of waiting can compress into decisions that need to be made carefully, not quickly. Mortgage drawdown timing is the second completion-stage decision that needs active management rather than passive acceptance of whatever the developer's timeline dictates. A buyer's rate lock secured at presale signing has an expiry date that may or may not still be valid by actual completion, especially if the project has slipped; confirming the rate lock's status well before handover, and understanding the re-lock terms if it has expired, avoids a last-minute scramble at a materially worse rate. A buyer relying on rental income to help service the new mortgage should time their existing tenant's lease-up or their own move-out to align with the confirmed completion date rather than the originally advertised one, and build in a buffer for the gap between formal handover and the unit actually being ready to occupy or lease, which is routinely longer than the marketing timeline suggested.

房貸撥款時機是完工階段第二項需要主動管理、而不是被動接受建商時程安排的決策。買方在預售簽約時鎖定的利率有一個到期日，這個到期日到了實際完工時可能已失效、也可能仍然有效，尤其是專案有延遲的情況下；在交屋前及早確認利率鎖定的狀態，並理解一旦失效後的重新鎖定條件，能避免在最後一刻手忙腳亂地接受一個明顯更差的利率。一位依賴租金收入來幫忙償還新房貸的買方，應該把既有租客的租約進駐時間、或自己的搬離時間，對齊已確認的完工日期，而不是原本廣告的日期，並為正式點交與單位真正準備好可入住或出租之間的落差預留緩衝，這段落差往往比行銷時程所暗示的更長。

🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

A widely cited category of Japanese first-owner cases illustrates the structural-depreciation dynamic in practice: buyers of new detached houses in Japan's major metros routinely see the structure's assessed and market value decline toward a small residual within roughly two to three decades post-construction, even for well-maintained homes, according to Japanese real-estate industry data and academic housing research repeatedly cited in coverage of Japan's housing market. This is a market-wide, well-documented pattern rather than an isolated case, and it means that a first-owner buyer's total return depends overwhelmingly on land value appreciation (or depreciation) at the location, essentially decoupled from the value of the structure itself — a materially different economic model from most other developed housing markets, where the physical structure typically retains or gains value alongside the land over a multi-decade holding period. Investors moving capital into Japanese residential real estate for the first time have repeatedly cited this dynamic as the single most counterintuitive adjustment to their underwriting, precisely because it inverts an assumption ("newer is always worth more, forever") that holds in most other markets they've bought in.

一個廣為引用的日本首購屋主案例類別，實際說明了結構性折舊的動態：根據日本不動產業界資料與屢屢被日本住房市場報導引用的學術住房研究，日本主要都會區獨棟新屋的買方，通常會看到建築結構的估價與市值，在興建後大約二十到三十年內，逐漸下滑到一個很小的殘值，即使是維護良好的住宅也不例外。這是一個全市場性、有充分紀錄的模式，而不是孤立案例，代表首購屋主的總報酬絕大部分取決於該地點的土地價值增值（或貶值），基本上與建築結構本身的價值脫鉤——這跟多數其他已開發住房市場明顯不同，在那些市場裡，實體建築結構通常會在數十年的持有期間內，跟著土地一起保值或增值。首度將資金投入日本住宅不動產的投資人一再指出，這個動態是他們核算過程中最違反直覺的一項調整，正是因為它顛覆了一個在他們曾買過的多數其他市場都成立的假設（「越新的房子永遠越值錢」）。
