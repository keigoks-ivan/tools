Household A earns a combined $140,000 a year and currently rents a two-bedroom apartment for $2,400 a month. A three-bedroom townhouse three blocks away is listed at $480,000. Putting 20% down means writing a $96,000 check and financing the remaining $384,000 with a 30-year mortgage at a prevailing rate of 6.5%. Mortgage principal and interest come to roughly $2,430 a month; add property tax, insurance and a maintenance reserve and the all-in monthly carrying cost lands around $2,980.

A 家庭年收入合計 14 萬美元，目前租住一間兩房公寓，月租 2,400 美元。三個街區外有一間三房連棟屋，開價 48 萬美元。付 20% 頭期款代表要拿出 9.6 萬美元現金，剩下 38.4 萬美元以 30 年期房貸融資，現行利率 6.5%。房貸本息大約每月 2,430 美元；加上房產稅、保險與維護準備金，每月全部持有成本落在約 2,980 美元左右。

$2,980 versus $2,400 looks like a $580 monthly premium for owning. That gap — not an abstract definition of "real estate investing" — is the actual question this course exists to unpack, starting here: what is Household A really buying with those extra dollars, and why is the answer to that question analyzed so differently for a home than for a commercial building generating rent for someone else? Most of that gap, it turns out, is not a cost at all. 1. How Residential Is Priced: Comparables, Not Income

一、住宅如何定價：靠比較法，不靠收入

A commercial building is priced primarily off its net operating income — the market converts a verifiable stream of rent into a price using a cap rate, and a buyer who cannot make that math work simply does not transact. A home is priced almost entirely differently: by comparablesRecently sold properties similar in size, condition, location and features to the subject property, used to estimate what it should sell for — the dominant valuation method for residential real estate, as opposed to an income-based valuation.與標的物在坪數、屋況、地點與條件上相似的近期成交案例，用來估計標的物應有的成交價——這是住宅不動產最主要的估值方法，相對於以收入為基礎的估值。 — what similar homes nearby recently sold for, adjusted for condition, square footage, bedroom count and location. Most residential buyers never run a yield calculation at all. They are comparing Household A's townhouse to the one two doors down that sold for $472,000 last spring, not to what a tenant would pay to rent it.

商用建築主要依淨營運收益定價——市場用資本化率把一筆可驗證的租金收入流換算成價格，買方如果這套算式跑不出合理報酬，交易根本不會成立。住宅的定價方式幾乎完全不同：靠的是可比成交案例Recently sold properties similar in size, condition, location and features to the subject property, used to estimate what it should sell for — the dominant valuation method for residential real estate, as opposed to an income-based valuation.與標的物在坪數、屋況、地點與條件上相似的近期成交案例，用來估計標的物應有的成交價——這是住宅不動產最主要的估值方法，相對於以收入為基礎的估值。——附近類似住宅最近的成交價，再依屋況、坪數、房間數與地點做調整。大多數住宅買方根本從未跑過任何殖利率計算。他們比較的是 A 家庭的連棟屋跟兩戶之外那間去年春天以 47.2 萬美元成交的房子，而不是跟「租客願意付多少租金」相比。

This has a precise mechanical explanation, and it comes down to who actually sets the clearing price in any market: not the average buyer, but the marginal one — the last, most motivated buyer whose bid clears the transaction. In a market dominated by owner-occupiers, the marginal buyer is usually someone purchasing consumption alongside investment: a specific school district, a specific commute, the certainty of not being asked to move by a landlord. That buyer will rationally pay more than a pure rental-yield investor would, because part of what they are buying — the utility of living there — never shows up in a rent roll at all. The result is a structural, recurring pattern: a market can look "expensive" by a rental-yield lens for years without that yield gap ever closing, because the yield lens is measuring the wrong buyer's motivation.

這背後有一個精確的機制解釋，關鍵在於任何市場真正決定成交價的，從來不是平均買方，而是邊際買方——那個出價剛好讓交易成立的、最有動機的最後一位買方。在一個由自住買方主導的市場裡，邊際買方通常同時在買消費與投資：特定的學區、特定的通勤距離、不會被房東要求搬走的確定性。這位買方理性上會願意付出比純租金殖利率投資人更高的價格，因為他所購買的一部分價值——住在那裡的效用——從來不會出現在租金明細表裡。結果是一個結構性、反覆出現的現象：某個市場用租金殖利率的角度看可能顯得「太貴」，而且這個殖利率缺口可能好幾年都不會收斂，因為殖利率這個角度，本來就衡量錯了買方的真正動機。

2. Financing, Tenant Law, and Liquidity: The Other Structural Differences

二、融資、房客保護法規與流動性：其他結構性差異

Individuals can typically borrow far more, and far more cheaply relative to their income, against a home than against almost any other asset class. Residential mortgage markets in many countries offer amortization periods of 25–30 years, down payment minimums well below what an equity investor would ever accept as margin, and in many cases government-backed insurance or guarantee programs that let lenders extend credit they otherwise would not. Commercial lending runs on a completely different logic: a lender underwrites the property's own income, typically requiring a debt service coverage ratio and a loan-to-value ceiling anchored to verified in-place rent, not to the borrower's personal income at all. The specific down payment minimums, amortization terms, and available government programs vary enormously by jurisdiction, and none of the figures above should be assumed to apply to a specific market without checking local rules directly (varies by jurisdiction).

相對於幾乎所有其他資產類別，個人通常能以住宅為擔保、用遠高於自身收入比例的額度、且成本遠更低廉地借到錢。許多國家的住宅房貸市場提供 25 至 30 年的攤還期、遠低於股票投資人所能接受融資保證金水準的最低頭期款，而且在許多情況下還有政府擔保或保險計畫，讓放款方願意承作原本不會承作的信用額度。商用貸款則完全是另一套邏輯：放款方核保的是物業本身的收入，通常要求以已驗證的現況租金為錨的償債保障比與貸款成數上限，而完全不看借款人的個人收入。上述具體的最低頭期款比例、攤還年限與可用的政府計畫，會因司法轄區而有極大差異，任何一個數字都不應該未經查證當地規則就套用到特定市場（依司法轄區而異）。

Residential real estate is also the asset class where tenant protection lawStatutory rules governing eviction procedures, notice periods, and in some jurisdictions rent increases, that apply to residential landlord-tenant relationships — often more restrictive than commercial lease terms, which are individually negotiated contracts between sophisticated parties.規範驅逐程序、通知期限，在部分司法轄區還包括租金調漲的法定規則，適用於住宅房東與房客的關係——往往比商用租約更具限制性，因為商用租約是雙方具備專業能力、逐一協商出來的合約。 most directly shapes a landlord's actual economics, in a way that has no close equivalent in most commercial leasing. Rent control regimes, mandatory notice periods, restrictions on no-cause eviction, and habitability requirements are structural features of the residential asset class rather than one-off contract terms, because a commercial lease is a bilaterally negotiated agreement between two sophisticated parties while a residential lease is governed on top of that by statute designed to protect the weaker party in a housing relationship. The specific rules — what counts as just cause for eviction, whether rent increases are capped, how long a notice period must be — differ sharply by city and country and change over time (varies by jurisdiction), and they belong on any landlord's underwriting checklist before a purchase, not after.

Liquidity for residential real estate is genuinely paradoxical. A single-family home or one condo unit typically transacts in weeks to a few months, backed by an enormous pool of retail buyers — nothing like the thin, specialized buyer pool a large commercial asset depends on, which can take many months or longer to find a matching institutional buyer. Yet despite that deep pool, residential transactions carry high friction per trade: agent commissions, transfer taxes or stamp duty, legal fees and lender fees typically add up to several percent of the purchase price on each side, round-trip costs that a large commercial deal often absorbs at a much lower proportional rate given its size. Residential real estate is liquid in the sense of "many buyers, fast closings" and illiquid in the sense of "expensive to enter and exit" at the same time — both things are true, and both belong in how a purchase decision gets underwritten.

住宅不動產的流動性其實是矛盾的。一間獨立屋或一戶公寓通常在數週到幾個月內就能成交，背後有龐大的散戶買方群體撐腰——完全不像大型商用資產所仰賴的那種薄且高度專業化的買方池，後者可能要花上許多個月甚至更久才能找到匹配的機構買家。然而儘管買方池深厚，住宅交易的每一筆單位摩擦成本卻很高：仲介佣金、交易稅或印花稅、律師費與貸款相關費用，雙方合計通常會達到成交價的好幾個百分點，而這種來回交易成本，大型商用交易由於規模效應，占比往往低得多。住宅不動產同時具備「買方多、成交快」意義上的流動性，以及「進出成本高」意義上的不流動性——這兩件事都是真的，也都應該納入購屋決策的核算之中。

Worked Example — Separating Imputed Rent From True Cost逐步演算範例——把設算租金從真實成本中拆分出來
  1. 1 Assume Household A's only alternative is renting an equivalent unit at $2,400/month — this is the imputed rent: the value of shelter Household A consumes every month by owning, whether or not any cash actually changes hands. 假設 A 家庭唯一的替代方案是以 $2,400/月 租一間等值的單位——這就是設算租金：A 家庭每個月因為自住所消費的居住價值，不論實際上是否有現金易手。
  2. 2 Break the $2,980 monthly carrying cost into its parts: mortgage interest ≈ $2,080, mortgage principal (equity build) ≈ $350, property tax + insurance + maintenance reserve ≈ $550. 把每月 2,980 美元的持有成本拆解成幾個部分：房貸利息約 $2,080，房貸本金（權益累積）約 $350，房產稅＋保險＋維護準備金約 $550。
  3. 3 The $350 principal portion is not consumption at all — it converts directly into home equity Household A owns outright, so it behaves like forced savings, not a housing cost. 這 350 美元的本金部分根本不是消費——它會直接轉換成 A 家庭完全擁有的房屋權益，所以它的性質比較像強制儲蓄，而不是居住成本。
  4. 4 Subtracting both the imputed rent (value already received) and the principal paydown (savings, not cost) from the $2,980 headline: $2,980 − $2,400 − $350 = $230/month — the true incremental consumption cost of owning versus renting this specific pair of properties at these specific prices and rates, a very different number from the naive "$580 more expensive" comparison. 從 2,980 美元的表面數字中，同時扣掉設算租金（已經取得的價值）與本金攤還（儲蓄，非成本）：$2,980 − $2,400 − $350 = $230/月——這才是在這組特定價格與利率下，自住相對於租屋真正增加的消費成本，跟粗略的「每月多花 580 美元」相比是完全不同的數字。

3. The Return Equation: Imputed Rent, Cash Rent, and Two Kinds of Markets

三、報酬方程式：設算租金、現金租金，與兩種市場

For an owner-occupier, total return has three components: price appreciation, imputed rent, minus carrying costs — the worked example above just walked through exactly that decomposition. For a landlord, the equation is simpler and fully denominated in cash: rent actually collected, plus appreciation, minus operating expenses, minus financing cost. The two return equations describe the same physical asset, but an owner-occupier is capturing part of their return as consumption they never see as a cash inflow, while a landlord's entire return shows up as a number on a bank statement (or doesn't). This is why the same building can make perfect sense for one buyer type and marginal sense for the other, at the identical purchase price.

對自住買方而言，總報酬有三個組成部分：價格增值、設算租金，減去持有成本——上面的逐步演算範例正是走過了這個拆解過程。對房東而言，方程式更簡單、而且完全以現金計價：實際收到的租金，加上增值，減去營運費用，再減去融資成本。這兩套報酬方程式描述的是同一個實體資產，但自住買方所取得的一部分報酬，是他們永遠不會看到現金流入的消費，而房東的整體報酬則完全會顯示在銀行對帳單上（或不會顯示）。這就是為什麼同一棟建築物、同一個成交價格，對某一類買方而言完全合理，對另一類買方而言卻只是勉強合理的原因。

Zoom out from any single household and the same split shows up at the market level. Some cities and countries trade primarily on rental yield — prices are anchored to what a property can actually earn as rent, and a buyer can cross-check a listing's price against a plausible cash return. Other markets trade almost purely on appreciation expectation — current rental yield is thin, sometimes below the cost of financing, and the price is supported almost entirely by the belief that a future buyer will pay more, not by what the property earns today. Neither regime is inherently "correct"; they are different, real, observable equilibria, and which one a specific city currently sits in changes over time as rates, supply and buyer composition shift. The 84-city dashboard84 城儀表板 tracks which markets currently trade on yield versus which trade almost purely on appreciation expectation, and is the first place to check before assuming either framing applies to a given city.

把視角從單一家庭拉遠，同樣的分裂也會出現在市場層級。有些城市與國家主要以租金殖利率為定價基礎——價格錨定在物業實際能賺到的租金上，買方可以拿掛牌價格去核對一個合理的現金報酬率。另一些市場則幾乎純粹以增值預期為定價基礎——目前的租金殖利率很薄，有時甚至低於融資成本，價格幾乎完全靠「未來會有買方願意出更高價」這種信念撐著，而不是靠物業今天賺到的收入。這兩種型態本身沒有孰對孰錯；它們是不同的、真實存在、可觀察到的均衡狀態，而某個特定城市當下處於哪一種型態，會隨著利率、供給與買方組成的變化而改變。84 城儀表板84 城儀表板會持續追蹤目前哪些市場以殖利率定價、哪些幾乎純粹以增值預期定價，在假設任何一種框架適用於某個城市之前，這是第一個該查證的地方。

The distinction is not cosmetic — it changes what a valuation floor even is. A yield-driven market has an anchor: if the price falls far enough below what rent can plausibly support, cash buyers and investors step in and the decline is cushioned by that income floor. An appreciation-driven market has no such anchor. Its price is supported only by the expectation of the next buyer, which makes it structurally more reflexive — self-reinforcing on the way up, and with nothing but sentiment to arrest a decline on the way down.

這個區別並不只是表面差異——它改變的是「估值下限」本身是否存在。殖利率驅動的市場有一個錨：如果價格跌到遠低於租金所能合理支撐的水準，現金買方與投資人就會進場承接，租金收入形成的下限會緩衝跌勢。增值驅動的市場則沒有這樣的錨。它的價格完全靠「下一位買方」的預期撐著，這使它在結構上更具反身性——上漲時自我強化，下跌時卻沒有任何東西能阻止，只剩下市場情緒。

4. Property Types and Their Economics

四、物件類型與其經濟結構

The same $1 of purchase price behaves differently depending on what kind of residential product it buys. Land share of value, management intensity and typical tenant type all shift meaningfully across property types, even within the same city.

同樣一塊錢的購買價格，會因為買到的是哪一種住宅產品而表現出完全不同的行為。土地占價值的比重、管理密集度與典型租客類型，即使在同一個城市裡，也會因物件類型而有明顯差異。

Condo / apartment unit — land value is shared across every unit in the building and diluted accordingly, so a condo's price tracks the building and the strata/HOA's condition far more than any land appreciation story. Management intensity for the individual owner is low — day-to-day upkeep is delegated to a body corporate or homeowners' association — but that also means the owner is exposed to collective decisions and special assessments they do not individually control.

公寓／集合住宅單位——土地價值由整棟大樓的所有單位共同分攤，因此被稀釋，公寓的價格走勢更多取決於整棟大樓與管委會／業主協會的狀況，而不是任何土地增值故事。對個別業主而言管理密集度低——日常維護委託給管委會或業主協會處理——但這也代表業主暴露在自己無法單獨掌控的集體決策與特別攤提之下。

Single-family detached house — land share of value is typically much higher, especially in supply-constrained locations, which is exactly why detached houses in land-scarce cities tend to show the strongest long-run appreciation-driven behavior. Management intensity falls entirely on the individual owner — roof, plumbing, landscaping — with no shared body to delegate to, which is a real ongoing cost and time commitment that a condo owner does not carry to the same degree.

獨立屋——土地占價值的比重通常高得多，尤其是在供給受限的地點，這正是為什麼土地稀缺城市裡的獨立屋，長期往往表現出最強的增值驅動特徵。管理密集度完全落在個別業主身上——屋頂、水電管線、庭院——沒有共同的組織可以委託，這是公寓業主不需要承擔到同等程度的、持續的成本與時間投入。

Townhouse — sits structurally between the two: some shared common-area maintenance (often through a smaller, lighter-weight association than a large condo tower) alongside a meaningful individual land share, typically a strip of land attached to each unit. It tends to inherit a blend of both economics rather than a distinct one of its own.

連棟屋——在結構上介於前兩者之間：有一部分共用區域的維護（通常透過比大型公寓大樓更輕量的協會處理），同時每戶也附帶一定比例的個別土地，通常是與每個單位相連的一小塊土地。它往往同時繼承兩者的部分經濟特徵，而不是自成一格。

Subdivided rental units — a single title split into several small rooms sharing a corridor and often shared bathroom or kitchen facilities, common in some Asian rental markets — trades management intensity for aggregate yield. Renting the same floor area as five or six small rooms to five or six separate tenants typically produces more total monthly rent than renting it as one whole unit to one tenant, but it multiplies tenant turnover, maintenance calls and the administrative burden of collecting rent from several unrelated parties instead of one. It is a real and common strategy for maximizing cash yield on a fixed floor area, at the direct cost of the landlord's time.

分租套房（走廊套房）——單一產權分割成數個小房間、共用走廊與往往共用衛浴或廚房設施，在部分亞洲租屋市場中很常見——用管理密集度換取總租金收益。同樣一塊樓地板面積，分成五、六間小房分別租給五、六位租客，通常會比整層租給單一租客產生更高的總月租金，但也會讓租客更替、維修叫修與向多位互不相干的租客收租的行政負擔倍增。這是把固定樓地板面積的現金殖利率極大化的一個真實且常見的策略，代價是房東直接付出的時間成本。

Shophouse / mixed-use — ground-floor commercial space with residential floors above, common across Southeast and East Asia — blends two different economics under one title. The commercial ground floor is priced closer to a small-scale CRE asset, driven by foot traffic, commercial tenant demand and business viability, while the residential floors above behave more like ordinary housing. A shophouse's value depends on both halves working, and either half underperforming — a vacant shop, or a poor residential layout above it — drags on the whole asset.

店屋（混合用途）——一樓為商用空間、樓上為住宅樓層，在東南亞與東亞相當常見——在單一產權下混合了兩種不同的經濟結構。一樓商用空間的定價方式更接近小型商用不動產，取決於人流、商用租戶需求與生意能否經營下去，而樓上的住宅樓層則表現得更像一般住宅。店屋的價值取決於兩個部分都要運作良好，任何一半表現不佳——空置的店面，或樓上格局不理想——都會拖累整個資產。

5. Tenure and Vintage: Leasehold vs Freehold, New vs Old Stock

五、產權與屋齡：租賃地權對永久地權、新屋對舊屋

New construction and older stock carry a fundamentally different maintenance liability profile, and the difference is really about who bears that liability, not just how new the paint looks. A newly built property usually comes with a developer warranty covering structural and major systems defects for a set number of years, so the buyer's near-term maintenance exposure is genuinely lower. As a building ages past that warranty window, the maintenance liability transfers fully onto the owner (or the collective body of owners, for a strata property) — roofs, plumbing, electrical systems and building envelopes all have finite service lives, and a building's price should reflect where it sits on that liability curve, not just its curb appeal.

新建物與舊屋在維護責任的輪廓上有根本性的差異，而這個差異真正關乎的是誰要承擔這份責任，而不只是油漆看起來有多新。新建物通常附帶開發商保固，涵蓋結構與主要系統缺陷、為期若干年，因此買方近期的維護風險確實較低。一旦建築物過了保固期限，維護責任就完全轉移到業主身上（若是分層產權物業，則轉移到全體業主的集體身上）——屋頂、水電管線、電力系統與建築外殼都有各自有限的使用年限，物業的價格應該反映它目前落在這條責任曲線上的哪個位置，而不只是外觀好不好看。

Tenure structure changes the underlying math in a way that goes beyond maintenance. 6. The Participant Map: Agents, Brokers, Appraisers, and Other Roles

六、參與者地圖：仲介、貸款經紀、估價師與其他角色

A residential transaction involves several parties whose interests are only partly aligned with the buyer's, and understanding each one's actual incentive — not just their job title — is a prerequisite to reading anything else in this course correctly.

一筆住宅交易牽涉到好幾個利益只與買方部分一致的參與者，弄清楚每一個人真正的誘因——而不只是他們的職稱——是正確理解這門課後續內容的前提。

A buyer's agent and a seller's (listing) agent are typically paid a commission calculated as a percentage of the sale price, which structurally rewards a deal happening quickly and at a workable price far more than it rewards squeezing out the last few percent in the buyer's or seller's favor — a percentage-based commission on a marginal price difference is small compared to the commission an agent forgoes entirely if the deal falls through. Mortgage brokers may be compensated by the lender, the borrower, or a mix of both depending on the jurisdiction and the specific arrangement, and a broker paid more by steering a borrower toward a particular lender's product has an incentive that does not automatically point toward the loan that is actually cheapest for that borrower over its full term. Appraisers are paid by the buyer or the lender but are structurally meant to be independent of both — their job is to produce a value opinion nobody involved in the transaction is allowed to pressure, which sets up a real tension explored further in Module 4: an appraisal that lags a fast-moving market, or comes under pressure to "hit the number" a deal needs to close, is one of the more persistent quality problems in residential valuation. Inspectors are paid by the buyer, which aligns their incentive reasonably well with the buyer's — but they are frequently referred to the buyer by the buyer's own agent, who has their own reasons to want the deal to close smoothly, a softer, indirect version of the same conflict. Property managers work for the landlord and are typically compensated as a percentage of collected rent, which usually aligns them with keeping units occupied, though not always with keeping maintenance costs as low as a hands-on owner might prefer. Developers selling new stock control both the initial pricing and, in many pre-sale markets, the pace and content of disclosure to early buyers — a structurally different position from a single individual seller, since a developer is pricing an entire project's absorption curve, not just one unit.

貸款經紀人的報酬可能來自放款方、借款人，或依司法轄區與具體安排而定的兩者混合，一位因為引導借款人使用某放款方特定產品而獲得更高報酬的經紀人，其誘因並不會自動指向對該借款人整個貸款期間而言真正最划算的貸款。估價師的費用由買方或放款方支付，但在結構上應該對雙方都保持獨立——他們的工作是產出一個不受交易中任何一方施壓的價值意見，這也帶出第四課會進一步探討的真實張力：一份落後於快速變動市場的估價，或是被施壓要「湊出交易成交所需的數字」，是住宅估值中比較持續存在的品質問題之一。驗屋師由買方付費，這讓他們的誘因與買方大致一致——但他們經常是由買方自己的仲介轉介而來，而仲介自己有理由希望交易順利成交，這是同一種利益衝突比較溫和、間接的版本。物業管理公司為房東工作，通常按收取租金的一定比例計酬，這通常會讓他們的誘因與維持出租率一致，但不一定與親自管理的業主可能更希望壓低的維修成本一致。銷售新成屋的開發商同時掌控初始定價，在許多預售市場中，也掌控向早期買方揭露資訊的節奏與內容——這與單一個人賣方的處境有結構性差異，因為開發商定價的是整個專案的去化曲線，而不只是單一戶。

7. Politically Protected, Politically Constrained

七、政治保護與政治制約

Owner-occupiers make up an electoral majority in most developed democracies, and that single fact pulls housing policy in two opposite directions at once, not one. In the protective direction: policies that would risk crashing home values tend to face heavy political resistance, because a majority of voters are homeowners with a direct financial stake in prices staying high or rising. Zoning restrictions that limit new supply near existing neighborhoods, tax breaks specifically for owner-occupiers, and resistance to any policy framed as "reducing property values" all draw on this same protective instinct — existing owners voting, in effect, to protect the asset most of their net worth sits in.

在大多數已開發民主國家，自住業主占選民的多數，而這個單一事實會把住宅政策同時往兩個相反的方向拉扯，而不是只有一個方向。在保護的方向上：任何有可能讓房價崩跌的政策，往往會遭遇強大的政治阻力，因為多數選民都是房屋所有人，對房價維持高檔或持續上漲有直接的財務利害關係。限制既有社區附近新供給的分區規劃限制、專門給自住業主的租稅優惠，以及對任何被貼上「降低房產價值」標籤的政策的抗拒，都源自同一種保護本能——現有業主投票，實質上就是在保護自己淨資產中占最大比重的那項資產。

In the opposite direction, the same electoral majority produces recurring anti-speculation policy whenever affordability becomes politically salient — typically aimed squarely at investors and non-resident buyers rather than at existing homeowners themselves. Foreign buyer taxes, additional stamp duty on second homes, and restrictions on short-term rentals are the standard toolkit, and they work politically precisely because taxing an outside buyer segment protects existing owner-voters' own asset values while being framed as addressing affordability for those same voters' children — protection and constraint pursued through the same policy at the same time, aimed at different targets. The exact mechanisms — which buyer categories get taxed, at what rate, in which regions — vary enormously by jurisdiction and shift with the political cycle (varies by jurisdiction).

在相反的方向上，同一群選民多數，一旦負擔能力成為政治上敏感的議題，就會反覆催生出反投機政策——通常直接針對投資人與非本地買方，而不是針對現有屋主本身。外國買方稅、針對第二戶住宅加徵的印花稅，以及對短期出租的限制，是標準的政策工具箱，而它們之所以在政治上行得通，正是因為對外部買方群體課稅，一方面保護了現有業主選民自身的資產價值，另一方面又可以被包裝成在解決這些選民子女所面對的負擔能力問題——保護與制約在同一時間、透過同一項政策，被同時追求，只是瞄準了不同的對象。具體的機制——哪些買方類別會被課稅、稅率多少、適用哪些地區——會因司法轄區而有極大差異，也會隨政治週期而變動（依司法轄區而異）。

🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

In August 2016, the government of British Columbia, Canada, introduced a 15% Additional Property Transfer Tax targeted specifically at foreign national buyers purchasing residential property in the Metro Vancouver region, at a time when rapid home price increases had become one of the province's dominant political issues. The tax was raised to 20% in February 2018 and its geographic scope later expanded to additional regions of the province. The episode is a clean illustration of the constraint side of the protection-and-constraint tension: a government whose electoral base was overwhelmingly existing homeowners still moved decisively against a specific buyer segment — foreign nationals — once affordability pressure became politically unavoidable, precisely because doing so let the government be seen protecting the same voters' housing wealth and their children's ability to eventually buy, at the same time.

2016 年 8 月，加拿大卑詩省（British Columbia）政府引進一項 15% 的額外物業轉讓稅，專門針對在大溫哥華地區購買住宅物業的外國籍買方，當時房價快速上漲已經成為該省最主要的政治議題之一。這項稅率在 2018 年 2 月調升至 20%，其適用地理範圍後續也擴大到該省其他地區。這個案例清楚說明了保護與制約張力中「制約」的那一面：一個選民基礎絕大多數是現有屋主的政府，一旦負擔能力壓力在政治上已無法迴避，仍然果斷地針對一個特定買方群體——外國籍人士——採取行動，原因正是這樣做能讓政府同時被視為在保護同一群選民的房產財富、以及他們子女未來購屋的能力。

  2. 2 Assume the home appreciates 3% over the next year, and the loan balance is roughly unchanged: $480,000 × 1.03 = $494,400. Equity = $494,400 − $384,000 = $110,400. 假設房屋在接下來一年增值 3%，貸款餘額大致不變：$480,000 × 1.03 = $494,400。權益＝$494,400 − $384,000 = $110,400。
  3. 3 Equity grew from $96,000 to $110,400 — a gain of $14,400 ÷ $96,000 = 15.0%, roughly five times the 3% underlying price move, because the appreciation applies to the full $480,000 asset while the gain is measured against only the $96,000 actually invested. 權益從 9.6 萬美元成長到 11.04 萬美元——增幅為 $14,400 ÷ $96,000 = 15.0%，約為 3% 標的價格漲幅的五倍，因為增值是套用在整棟 48 萬美元的資產上，但增幅的計算基礎卻只是實際投入的 9.6 萬美元。
  4. 4 Now reverse it: the same home falls 3% instead of rising. $480,000 × 0.97 = $465,600. Equity = $465,600 − $384,000 = $81,600 — a loss of $14,400, or 15.0% of the original $96,000 equity, again roughly five times the 3% price decline. 現在反過來：同一間房子不是上漲、而是下跌 3%。$480,000 × 0.97 = $465,600。權益＝$465,600 − $384,000 = $81,600——虧損 $14,400，等於原本 9.6 萬美元權益的 15.0%，同樣約為 3% 價格跌幅的五倍。
  5. 5 The mechanism does not stop scaling at 15%. Equity reaches exactly zero when the price falls enough to equal the loan balance: ($480,000 − $384,000) ÷ $480,000 = 20%. Beyond a 20% decline, Household A would owe more on the mortgage than the home is worth — negative equityA situation where the outstanding mortgage balance exceeds the current market value of the home, meaning the owner's equity is below zero — common among highly leveraged buyers when home prices fall sharply.未償還房貸餘額超過房屋目前市值的狀態，代表業主的權益低於零——在房價大跌時，常見於槓桿程度較高的買方身上。 — even though the underlying asset itself lost "only" a fifth of its value. A buyer who put down less than 20% reaches negative equity at an even smaller price decline. 這個機制不會在 15% 就停止放大。權益會在價格下跌到剛好等於貸款餘額時歸零：($480,000 − $384,000) ÷ $480,000 = 20%。一旦跌幅超過 20%，A 家庭欠銀行的房貸金額就會超過房屋的市值——也就是負權益A situation where the outstanding mortgage balance exceeds the current market value of the home, meaning the owner's equity is below zero — common among highly leveraged buyers when home prices fall sharply.未償還房貸餘額超過房屋目前市值的狀態，代表業主的權益低於零——在房價大跌時，常見於槓桿程度較高的買方身上。——即使標的資產本身「只」跌了五分之一。頭期款低於 20% 的買方，會在更小的跌幅就陷入負權益。
🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

Through the mid-2000s, mortgage underwriting standards in the United States loosened substantially: low- and no-down-payment loans, limited income documentation, and adjustable rates that reset upward after an initial low-rate period all became widespread. A large share of buyers entered ownership with leverage far higher than the 5x illustrated above — some effectively 10x, 20x or more relative to the cash they actually put in. When national home prices turned and fell through roughly 2006–2012, widely cited indices show a decline on the order of a quarter to a third from the mid-2000s peak — well past the 20% threshold that wipes out a 20%-down buyer's equity entirely, and reached almost immediately for buyers who had put down 5% or less. Millions of homeowners ended up owing more on their mortgages than their homes were worth, and foreclosure filings surged into the millions annually at the crisis's peak. The mechanism was exactly the reverse-leverage arithmetic worked through above, compounded across an entire national housing stock at once instead of one hypothetical household.

2000 年代中期，美國的房貸核保標準大幅放寬：低頭期款甚至零頭期款貸款、寬鬆的收入證明要求，以及在初期低利率期過後會向上重設的浮動利率，全都變得相當普遍。很大一部分買方以遠高於前述 5 倍的槓桿進場自住——有些人實質槓桿高達 10 倍、20 倍甚至更高，相對於他們實際投入的現金而言。當全美房價在約 2006 年至 2012 年間反轉下跌時，廣為引用的指數顯示，從 2000 年代中期的高點算起，跌幅約落在四分之一到三分之一之間——這已經遠遠超過讓一位頭期款 20% 的買方權益完全歸零的 20% 門檻，而對於頭期款只付了 5% 或更少的買方而言，這個門檻幾乎立刻就會被觸及。數以百萬計的屋主最終欠銀行的房貸金額超過了房屋本身的市值，在危機最高峰時，每年的法拍申請案件數量飆升到數百萬件之譜。這正是前面演算過的槓桿反向算式，只是同時在全國整體住宅存量上疊加放大，而不只是發生在一個假設的家庭身上。

9. Owner-Occupier First — And When Not to Buy

九、自住優先——以及什麼時候不該買

A first home is best understood as a consumption-and-investment hybrid, not a pure investment, which is exactly why it gets analyzed with a different toolkit than a rental property or a commercial building. The buyer is purchasing shelter they would have to pay for anyway — via imputed rent, as worked through earlier — plus an investment position in the same asset, at the same time, in a single transaction. Treating it as purely an investment and judging it solely against a stock market return misses the imputed-rent half of the return entirely; treating it as purely consumption and ignoring the investment exposure entirely misses the other half. Both errors are common, and both lead to bad decisions.

第一間房子最好理解為消費與投資的混合體，而不是純粹的投資，這正是為什麼它需要用一套跟出租物業或商用建築不同的工具來分析。買方買的是原本就必須支付的居住空間——透過前面演算過的設算租金——同時也在同一筆交易中，同時取得同一項資產的投資部位。如果純粹把它當投資、只拿股市報酬來評判，就完全忽略了報酬中設算租金的那一半；如果純粹把它當消費、完全忽略投資曝險，則忽略了另一半。這兩種錯誤都很常見，也都會導致糟糕的決策。

There are specific, recurring situations where the math tilts toward renting instead. A short expected holding period is the clearest one: the transaction costs worked through earlier — commissions, transfer taxes, legal fees — on both the purchase and the eventual sale can easily eat several percent of the property's value, a round-trip cost Module 2 quantifies in detail, and a holding period too short to let appreciation and imputed rent outweigh that friction makes buying a poor decision regardless of how attractive the property itself looks. Unstable income given the asset's illiquidity is another: a home cannot be sold in days the way a stock position can if a job is lost or income drops, so committing to a large, hard-to-exit mortgage payment against uncertain future income concentrates risk exactly where a buyer can least afford it. A market priced heavily for appreciation the buyer cannot actually underwrite — thin or negative rental yield relative to financing cost, as covered in Section 3 — is a third: buying there means betting on a future buyer paying more, not on anything the property itself demonstrably earns. And in a given local yield environment, the arithmetic of renting and separately investing the difference between rent and the total cost of owning can simply outperform buying, particularly in appreciation-driven markets where the imputed-rent return is not being compensated by a matching cash yield.

有幾種具體且反覆出現的情況，會讓算式的天平傾向租屋而非購屋。預期持有期間過短是最明顯的一種：前面提到的交易成本——佣金、交易稅、律師費——在購入與最終出售兩端都會發生，合計很容易吃掉物業價值的好幾個百分點，這筆來回成本第二課會詳細量化，如果持有期間短到不足以讓增值與設算租金蓋過這筆摩擦成本，那麼不論物件本身看起來多吸引人，購屋都會是一個糟糕的決策。考量到資產的不流動性，收入不穩定是另一種情況：房子不像股票部位那樣可以在失業或收入減少時幾天內就賣掉，因此在收入前景不確定的情況下，承諾一筆龐大且難以退出的房貸月付款，恰恰把風險集中在買方最無力承受的地方。一個價格幾乎完全建立在增值預期上、而買方根本無法核算其合理性的市場——如同第三節所述、租金殖利率相對融資成本偏薄甚至為負——是第三種情況：在這樣的市場買房，等於是在賭未來會有買方願意付更高的價格，而不是在賭物業本身有任何可驗證的獲利能力。而在某個特定的當地殖利率環境下，租屋、並把租金與購屋總成本之間的差額另外拿去投資的算式，很可能單純表現得比購屋更好，尤其是在增值驅動型市場——那裡設算租金這部分的報酬，並沒有相對應的現金殖利率作為補償。

⚠️
Pitfall:常見陷阱： A common framing error is judging a first home purely as a "good" or "bad" investment by comparing its price appreciation to a stock market return, while ignoring that a meaningful share of the true return was never meant to be cash at all — it is imputed rent, consumed as shelter every month whether or not the housing market moves. The mirror-image error is just as common: assuming rent is automatically "money thrown away" without actually running the imputed-rent-and-equity-build math for the specific price, rent and rate levels on offer, which can just as easily favor renting-and-investing-the-difference in a market priced heavily on appreciation expectation rather than yield — check the dashboard's yield-vs-appreciation read for the specific city before assuming either framing. 一個常見的框架錯誤，是純粹把第一間房子當成一項「好」或「壞」的投資，拿它的價格增值去跟股市報酬比較，卻忽略了真實報酬中有相當一部分，本來就不該以現金形式出現——那是設算租金，不論房市有沒有波動，每個月都以居住的形式被消費掉。反過來的錯誤同樣常見：不實際針對眼前的價格、租金與利率水準跑一次設算租金與權益累積的算式，就直接假設租屋是「白白把錢丟掉」——在增值預期主導、而非殖利率主導的市場裡，租屋並把差額另外拿去投資，很可能同樣划算，甚至更划算。在假設任何一種框架適用之前，先查一下儀表板對該城市殖利率對增值的判讀。
