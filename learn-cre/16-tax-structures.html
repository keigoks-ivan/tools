Depreciation: The Non-Cash Shield

折舊：非現金稅盾

A building's roof, HVAC system, and structure wear out — on paper, whether or not they wear out in real life that particular year. Most tax systems that touch real estate let an owner deduct a portion of the building's cost each year as a non-cash expense: no check is written, no cash leaves the operating account, but taxable income drops by that amount anyway. That gap — cash flow that is real, taxable income that is lower than cash flow — is the single most important structural feature of owning income real estate versus almost any other asset class.

建築物的屋頂、空調系統與結構體會折損——在帳面上如此，不論那一年它在現實中是否真的折損。大多數涉及不動產的稅制都容許業主每年把建築物成本的一部分列為非現金費用An expense that reduces taxable income without any cash actually leaving the business that year.一種會降低應稅所得、但當年實際上沒有現金流出的費用。來扣除：沒有開出支票，營運帳戶裡沒有現金流出，但應稅所得依然因此下降。這個落差——真實存在的現金流，卻低於它的應稅所得——是持有收益型不動產相對於幾乎任何其他資產類別，最重要的結構性特徵。

The mechanism only shields cash flow from tax for as long as the owner holds the asset. It doesn't erase the liability — it defers it, and attaches a price tag to the deferral: when the property is sold, most systems recaptureA rule that taxes some or all of the depreciation previously deducted, at the point of sale, often at a different (frequently higher) rate than ordinary capital gains.一種在出售時，把先前已扣除的部分或全部折舊金額重新課稅的規則，適用稅率經常不同於（且通常高於）一般資本利得稅率。 some or all of the depreciation previously claimed, taxing it — often at a rate higher than the ordinary capital-gains rate applied to the rest of the gain. The shield was real; so is the bill that eventually comes due.

這個機制只在業主持有該資產期間，把現金流從課稅中遮蔽起來。它並不會抹除這筆稅負——只是遞延，而且遞延是有代價的：出售時，大多數稅制會針對先前已申報的部分或全部折舊金額課徵折舊回收（recapture）A rule that taxes some or all of the depreciation previously deducted, at the point of sale, often at a different (frequently higher) rate than ordinary capital gains.一種在出售時，把先前已扣除的部分或全部折舊金額重新課稅的規則，適用稅率經常不同於（且通常高於）一般資本利得稅率。，且適用稅率經常高於其餘利得所適用的一般資本利得稅率。稅盾是真實的；最終要付的帳單也是真實的。

Two structural details matter more than the headline concept. First, land is typically not depreciable — only the building and its improvements — so the split between land value and building value at acquisition (an allocation, often contestable) determines how large the annual shield actually is. Second, some systems allow certain building components (elevators, parking lots, specific mechanical systems) to be depreciated faster than the building shell itself, front-loading the shield into the early years of ownership — a technique generically called component or cost-segregation depreciation, with its own compliance requirements that vary sharply by jurisdiction.

有兩個結構性細節，比這個大方向的概念本身更重要。第一，土地通常不可折舊——只有建築物與其改良物可以——因此收購時「土地價值」與「建築物價值」的拆分（一種常有爭議的分攤）決定了每年稅盾實際能有多大。第二，部分稅制容許某些建築物構件（電梯、停車場、特定機電系統）比建築物主體更快折舊完畢，把稅盾提前集中在持有初期——這種技術通稱為構件式或成本分離式折舊，其合規要求因司法轄區而異，差異相當大。

Interest Deductibility as a Policy Lever

利息扣抵：作為政策槓桿的工具

Most systems that allow interest to be deducted as a business expense are, structurally, subsidizing leverage — a dollar of interest expense reduces taxable income the same way a dollar of any other operating cost does, but interest only exists because the owner chose to borrow rather than use equity. This is not an accident; it's a policy choice, and policymakers treat it as a lever they can pull. When governments want to cool a credit cycle, restrict interest deductibility (cap it as a percentage of income, disallow it above a debt-to-equity ratio, or limit it for certain categories of lender) is a recurring tool — because it changes the after-tax cost of leverage without touching the headline interest rate at all.

大多數容許把利息列為營業費用扣除的稅制，在結構上其實是在補貼槓桿——一塊錢的利息費用，跟一塊錢的其他任何營運成本一樣會降低應稅所得，但利息之所以存在，是因為業主選擇了借款而非動用股本。這不是偶然，而是一項政策選擇，而政策制定者也把它當成一個可以拉動的槓桿。當政府想為信用週期降溫時，限制利息可扣抵性（把上限訂在所得的某個百分比、在債務對股本比超過某個門檻後不准扣除、或針對某些類別的放款人設限）就是一個反覆出現的工具——因為它能在完全不動用檯面上利率的情況下，改變槓桿的稅後成本。

The practical takeaway for an owner: interest-deductibility rules are not a fixed backdrop. A leveraged acquisition underwritten on today's deductibility regime can see its after-tax cost of debt rise meaningfully if a future budget cycle tightens the cap — with no change to the loan's contractual rate. This is one reason sophisticated buyers stress-test not just interest-rate scenarios but tax-policy scenarios when they size leverage on a long hold.

對業主而言，實務上的重點是：利息可扣抵規則不是固定不變的背景設定。一筆依據當下扣抵制度核保的槓桿收購案，如果未來某次預算週期收緊了扣抵上限，即使貸款合約利率完全沒變，稅後借款成本也可能明顯上升。這正是老練買家在為長期持有部位設定槓桿規模時，不只壓力測試利率情境，也壓力測試稅務政策情境的原因之一。

The Deferral Family: Like-Kind Exchanges

遞延家族：同類財產交換

A recurring pattern across tax systems is a mechanism that lets an owner sell one investment property and roll the proceeds into another, deferring the capital-gains tax that would otherwise be due — as long as specific conditions are met. The best-documented archetype is the United States' Section 1031 exchangeUS tax code provision allowing deferral of capital-gains tax when investment real estate is sold and the proceeds are reinvested into "like-kind" real estate under strict timing rules.美國稅法條款，容許在出售投資性不動產、並依嚴格時限規則將價款再投入「同類」不動產時，遞延資本利得稅。, formalized through decades of case law and regulation. Its mechanics are worth understanding in detail precisely because most other jurisdictions' rollover-relief regimes (where they exist at all) rhyme with the same underlying logic, even when their specific rules diverge sharply.

跨稅制反覆出現的一種模式，是容許業主出售一筆投資性不動產、並把價款再投入另一筆不動產，藉此遞延原本應繳的資本利得稅——前提是符合特定條件。文獻記載最完整的典型是美國的第 1031 條交換US tax code provision allowing deferral of capital-gains tax when investment real estate is sold and the proceeds are reinvested into "like-kind" real estate under strict timing rules.美國稅法條款，容許在出售投資性不動產、並依嚴格時限規則將價款再投入「同類」不動產時，遞延資本利得稅。，經過數十年的判例與法規逐步定型。深入理解它的機制是值得的，正因為大多數其他司法轄區的展延減免制度（如果存在的話）都跟同一套底層邏輯相呼應，即使具體規則差異很大。

The Mechanics: Timelines and Boot

機制：時限與找補款

A qualifying exchange runs on two hard clocks that start the day the relinquished property closes: 45 days to formally identify potential replacement properties, and 180 days total to close on the replacement. Miss either deadline and the deferral fails outright — the sale is taxed as if no exchange had ever been attempted. The proceeds must also pass through a qualified intermediary; the seller can never touch the cash directly, or the exchange is disqualified.

符合資格的交換案，從被出售資產成交日起，會啟動兩個硬性時鐘：45 天內須正式指認潛在的替代標的，以及總計 180 天內須完成替代標的的交割。任何一個期限沒趕上，遞延就整個失效——這筆交易會被當作根本沒有進行過交換，直接課稅。價款也必須經過合格中介人之手；賣方本人絕對不能直接碰到這筆現金，否則交換資格就會被取消。

If the replacement property is cheaper, or carries less debt, than the one sold, the difference — cash or debt relief received by the seller — is called bootNon-like-kind value (cash, or a reduction in debt) received in an exchange; it is taxed immediately, breaking the deferral for that portion.在交換中收到的非同類價值（現金，或債務減少），這部分會被立即課稅，等於打破了該部分的遞延。, and it is taxed immediately — the deferral only covers the portion genuinely rolled forward. This is why exchanges are structured to trade equal-or-up in both price and leverage: any step-down leaks tax.

如果替代標的的價格更低，或承接的債務更少，跟被出售的資產相比，這中間的差額——賣方收到的現金或債務減輕部分——稱為找補款（boot）Non-like-kind value (cash, or a reduction in debt) received in an exchange; it is taxed immediately, breaking the deferral for that portion.在交換中收到的非同類價值（現金，或債務減少），這部分會被立即課稅，等於打破了該部分的遞延。，會被立即課稅——遞延只覆蓋真正滾動延續下去的那一部分。這正是為什麼交換案的架構通常會設計成價格與槓桿都持平或往上走：任何一點往下調整，都會漏出應稅金額。

Why It Locks Capital Into the Asset Class

為什麼它會把資金鎖進同一種資產類別

The deferral only survives as long as the proceeds stay in like-kind real estate. Sell out of real estate into stocks, bonds, or a business — the deferred gain comes due in full. Over a career, an owner who exchanges repeatedly can end up with a large embedded, never-recognized gain riding on their current property, with no way to diversify out of real estate without triggering it. The gain doesn't disappear when the owner dies in most systems that offer this kind of deferral — see the estate section below — which is exactly why some owners treat "exchange until death" as a deliberate, decades-long tax strategy rather than a one-off transaction tool.

這種遞延只有在價款持續留在同類不動產內時才存在。一旦把不動產出售、轉進股票、債券或事業，遞延的利得就會全額到期應繳。經過一整段投資生涯，反覆進行交換的業主，最終可能在目前持有的物件上累積出一筆龐大、從未被實現認列的利得，卻沒有辦法在不觸發課稅的情況下把資金分散到不動產以外。在多數提供這類遞延的稅制下，業主過世時這筆利得通常不會憑空消失——見下方「遺產與傳承」段落——這正是為什麼有些業主會把「交換到過世為止」當成一項刻意規劃、長達數十年的稅務策略，而不只是單次交易工具。

🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

T.J. Starker v. United States (1979) is the case that gave the modern delayed exchange its legal foundation. Starker sold timberland to a lumber company but did not receive a replacement property simultaneously — instead, the buyer credited him with the sale value and agreed to acquire and transfer replacement properties to him over the following years. The IRS argued this was simply a sale followed by unrelated purchases, fully taxable. The Ninth Circuit disagreed, ruling that an exchange could be "delayed" and still qualify, as long as the replacement property was eventually received. Congress responded in 1984 by codifying the 45-day identification and 180-day closing windows precisely because the court had opened the door to exchanges stretched out indefinitely — the statute existed to bound a mechanism the case law had just legitimized.

1979 年的 T.J. Starker 訴美國政府案，是現代「延遲交換」取得法律基礎的關鍵判例。Starker 把林地賣給一家木材公司，但沒有同步取得替代標的——取而代之的是，買方將出售價值記在他名下，並同意在往後數年間陸續取得並移轉替代不動產給他。國稅局主張這實質上就是一筆出售，加上後續一連串不相關的購買行為，應全額課稅。第九巡迴上訴法院不同意，裁定只要最終確實取得了替代標的，交換即使被「延遲」進行，仍可符合資格。國會在 1984 年立法回應，正式訂入 45 天指認期與 180 天交割期兩道時限——之所以立法，正是因為這個判例已經打開了讓交換可以無限期拖延下去的大門，法規存在的目的就是為這個剛被判例正當化的機制劃出邊界。

Liability isolation. If one property is hit with a catastrophic lawsuit — a fatal accident, an environmental contamination claim — housing it in its own SPV means the claim, in principle, stops at that entity's balance sheet and cannot reach into the equity of the other properties in the portfolio. Lender bankruptcy-remoteness. A lender financing one asset wants certainty that the borrower entity cannot be dragged into a bankruptcy filing triggered by problems at a completely unrelated property the same sponsor owns — so loan agreements typically require the borrower to be a "bankruptcy-remote" SPV: single-purpose, restricted from taking on other debt, with independent directors whose consent is required before any voluntary bankruptcy filing. Without that isolation, cross-default and substantive-consolidation risk make the loan itself harder and more expensive to underwrite.

負債隔離。如果某一筆物件遭遇一起重大訴訟——致命意外事故、環境污染求償——把它安置在自己的 SPV 裡，代表這筆求償原則上會停在該實體的資產負債表上，無法波及投資組合裡其他物件的股本。放款人要求的破產隔離。為某一筆資產融資的放款人，希望確保借款實體不會因為同一發起人名下、完全無關的另一筆物件出問題，而被拖進破產程序——因此貸款合約通常要求借款方必須是「破產隔離」的 SPV：單一目的、不得再舉借其他債務、且須有獨立董事，任何自願聲請破產都須經其同意。少了這層隔離，交叉違約與實質合併求償的風險，會讓這筆貸款本身更難核保、成本也更高。

Where the Management Fee Flows

管理費流向哪裡

The holdco layer typically has a second job: it's where a management company (often a separate entity again, owned by the sponsor) contracts to provide asset-management and property-management services to each SPV, charging a fee that flows up from the property level to the sponsor. Structured this way, the fee is a deductible operating expense at the SPV level and taxable income to the management company — and in cross-border structures, this fee flow is exactly the kind of related-party transaction that tax authorities scrutinize most closely for transfer-pricing abuse (charging an artificially high management fee to shift profit out of a high-tax property jurisdiction into a lower-tax management-company jurisdiction).

控股公司這一層通常還有第二個功能：由一家管理公司（往往又是發起人名下的另一個獨立實體）與每個 SPV 訂約，提供資產管理與物業管理服務，收取的費用從物件層級往上流向發起人。用這種方式架構，這筆費用在 SPV 層級是可扣除的營運費用，在管理公司則是應稅所得——在跨境架構中，這種費用流動正是稅務機關最密切審查、以防移轉訂價濫用（藉由對高稅率不動產所在司法轄區收取人為偏高的管理費，把利潤移往稅率較低的管理公司所在司法轄區）的關聯人交易類型。

Pass-Through vs. Corporate Taxation

導管型課稅 vs. 公司型課稅

Two broad taxation models recur across jurisdictions for how these entities are taxed. In a pass-throughA structure where the entity itself pays no income tax; profits and losses flow through directly to the owners, who report them on their own returns.一種結構，實體本身不繳所得稅；損益直接流向業主，由業主在自己的申報中認列。 structure (US LLCs and limited partnerships are the archetype), the entity itself pays no income tax — profit and loss flow directly to the owners' own tax returns, avoiding a layer of taxation at the entity level. In a corporate structure, the entity pays tax on its profits, and owners pay tax again when profits are distributed to them as dividends — the classic "double taxation" concern that drives much of the structuring decision in the first place. Which model is available, and which is more efficient, depends entirely on the jurisdiction, the investor's own tax residency, and whether the investor is itself tax-exempt (a pension fund, for instance) — which is precisely why this decision is made with a tax advisor, not from a course module.

關於這些實體如何課稅，各司法轄區普遍出現兩大類模式。在導管型（pass-through）A structure where the entity itself pays no income tax; profits and losses flow through directly to the owners, who report them on their own returns.一種結構，實體本身不繳所得稅；損益直接流向業主，由業主在自己的申報中認列。結構中（美國的 LLC 與有限合夥是典型代表），實體本身不繳所得稅——損益直接流向業主自己的稅務申報，避開了實體層級的一層課稅。在公司型結構中，實體要就其利潤繳稅，而當利潤以股利形式分配給業主時，業主還要再繳一次稅——這正是所謂「雙重課稅」疑慮，也是驅動許多架構決策的根本原因。哪一種模式可用、哪一種較有效率，完全取決於司法轄區、投資人自身的稅務居住地，以及投資人本身是否為免稅實體（例如退休基金）——這正是為什麼這項決策要交給稅務顧問來做，而不是靠一堂課程模組。

Cross-Border Structural Frictions

跨境結構摩擦

Every layer of ownership that crosses a national border adds a friction point that a purely domestic structure never encounters. These are qualitative concepts here — the specific rates and thresholds are covered in Module 17 alongside the investment-strategy angle, and depend entirely on the jurisdictions involved.

每一層跨越國界的持有架構，都會多出一個純國內架構完全不會遇到的摩擦點。這裡談的是概念性的內容——具體稅率與門檻會在第 17 課、結合跨境投資策略的角度一併討論，且完全取決於所涉及的司法轄區。

Transfer Taxes and Stamp Duty: The Entry Cost That Changes the Hold-Period Math

移轉稅與印花稅：改變持有期算式的進場成本

Most jurisdictions charge a tax on the act of transferring real property itself — variously called stamp duty, transfer tax, or registration tax — assessed as a percentage of the purchase price and due at closing, regardless of whether the deal ever makes money. Unlike income tax, this cost is front-loaded and sunk the moment the deed records. Its practical effect is to raise the effective minimum hold period needed to break even, because a high entry tax must be amortized over the years the asset is held before it stops dragging on annualized return.

大多數司法轄區都會針對不動產移轉這個行為本身課稅——名稱各異，稱作印花稅、移轉稅或登記稅——按成交價的一定比例課徵，且在交割時就須繳納，不論這筆交易最終是否賺錢。跟所得稅不同，這筆成本是前置且沉沒的，房契一登記完成就確定了。它的實務效果，是提高損益兩平所需的有效最短持有期，因為一筆偏高的進場稅，必須攤提在持有的年數裡，才能不再拖累年化報酬率。

Worked Example — Stamp Duty and the Hold-Period Floor (假設 Hypothetical)逐步演算範例——印花稅與最短持有期底線（假設數字）
  1. 1 假設 Market A charges 1% stamp duty on purchase, Market B charges 8%. Both properties cost $10,000,000 and both are expected to appreciate 4%/yr and generate a stable 5% net cash yield. 假設 A 市場買方須繳 1% 印花稅，B 市場為 8%。兩筆物件成交價皆為 $10,000,000，預期年增值率皆為 4%，並產生穩定的 5% 淨現金殖利率。
  2. 2 Market A's entry tax: $10,000,000 × 1% = $100,000. Market B's: $10,000,000 × 8% = $800,000 — an $700,000 difference in sunk cost before either property has earned a cent. A 市場進場稅：$10,000,000 × 1% = $100,000。B 市場：$10,000,000 × 8% = $800,000——在任一物件開始賺到一分錢之前，沉沒成本就相差了 $700,000。
  3. 3 Spread evenly, Market A's tax costs about 0.2 percentage points of annualized return over a 5-year hold ($100,000 ÷ 5 ÷ $10,000,000). Market B's costs about 1.6 percentage points over the same 5 years — enough, on its own, to turn a marginally attractive deal unattractive. 若平均攤提在 5 年持有期，A 市場的稅負大約拖累年化報酬率 0.2 個百分點（$100,000 ÷ 5 ÷ $10,000,000）。B 市場在同樣 5 年內大約拖累 1.6 個百分點——單憑這一項，就足以把一筆邊際上還算吸引人的交易，變成不吸引人。

REIT Structure Tax Logic

REIT 結構的稅務邏輯

A publicly traded corporation that owns real estate would normally face the double-taxation problem described above: the corporation pays tax on its profits, then shareholders pay tax again on dividends. The REITReal Estate Investment Trust: a corporate structure that is exempt from entity-level income tax, in exchange for meeting statutory requirements — typically a minimum share of assets/income in real estate and a mandatory high percentage of taxable income distributed to shareholders annually.不動產投資信託：一種在符合法定條件下、可豁免實體層級所得稅的公司型結構——條件通常包括不動產資產／所得占比達最低標準，以及每年須將高比例應稅所得強制分配給股東。 structure, first created in the US in 1960 specifically to let ordinary investors access diversified, professionally managed real estate portfolios the way they could already access diversified stock portfolios through mutual funds, solves this by exempting the entity from income tax at the corporate level — in exchange for statutory conditions, chiefly a requirement to distribute a high percentage (often 90% or more) of taxable income to shareholders every year as dividends. The tax is not eliminated; it is simply pushed down to a single layer, paid by shareholders on the dividends they receive.

一家持有不動產的上市公司，通常會面臨前述雙重課稅問題：公司先就利潤繳稅，股東再就股利繳一次稅。REIT（不動產投資信託）Real Estate Investment Trust: a corporate structure that is exempt from entity-level income tax, in exchange for meeting statutory requirements — typically a minimum share of assets/income in real estate and a mandatory high percentage of taxable income distributed to shareholders annually.不動產投資信託：一種在符合法定條件下、可豁免實體層級所得稅的公司型結構——條件通常包括不動產資產／所得占比達最低標準，以及每年須將高比例應稅所得強制分配給股東。結構，最早於 1960 年在美國創設，目的正是讓一般投資人能像透過共同基金取得分散化股票組合一樣，取得分散化、專業管理的不動產組合——它的解法是把實體層級的所得稅豁免掉，交換條件是符合法定要求，主要是每年須把高比例（通常 90% 以上）的應稅所得以股利形式分配給股東。稅負並沒有被消除，只是被壓縮到單一層級，由股東就收到的股利繳納。

The distribution requirement is the mechanism's real teeth: it forces REITs to behave like income-distribution vehicles rather than tax-deferral machines, and it means REIT investors receive a steady, largely non-discretionary cash yield rather than management's choice of whether to retain earnings. This module's Section 3 discussed the deferral family; the REIT structure is, by design, the opposite instinct — distribute now, exempt the entity, tax the individual.

這項強制分配要求，正是這個機制真正的關鍵所在：它迫使 REIT 表現得像收益分配工具，而非稅務遞延機器，也意味著 REIT 投資人拿到的是穩定、且很大程度上不由管理階層自由裁量是否保留盈餘的現金殖利率。本課第三節談的是遞延家族；REIT 結構在設計上，走的正是相反的直覺——現在就分配、豁免實體層級、改為對個人課稅。

🎙 From the Trenches 來自實戰

The US Real Estate Investment Trust Act of 1960 was passed with an explicit populist framing: its sponsors argued that only the wealthy could assemble diversified real estate portfolios, while ordinary investors were shut out of an asset class that institutions and the rich had always used to build wealth. The REIT structure's entity-level tax exemption was the deliberate policy instrument used to make that access economically viable — without it, a REIT's returns to small shareholders would have been eroded by a corporate tax layer that a direct-owning millionaire never paid. Six decades later, publicly traded REITs collectively hold trillions of dollars of commercial real estate globally, and dozens of countries have since adopted their own REIT-equivalent regimes explicitly modeled on the same entity-exemption-for-distribution trade.

美國 1960 年通過的《不動產投資信託法》，帶著明確的平民主義訴求：提案者主張，只有富人才有能力組建分散化的不動產投資組合，而一般投資人則被排除在這個機構與富人一直用來累積財富的資產類別之外。REIT 結構的實體層級免稅，正是刻意設計來讓這種參與管道在經濟上可行的政策工具——若沒有這項豁免，REIT 分配給小股東的報酬，會被一層直接持有物件的富豪從來不用繳的公司稅侵蝕掉。六十多年後，全球上市 REIT 合計持有數兆美元的商用不動產，此後也有數十個國家陸續採用自己的 REIT 等效制度，明確參照同一套「以強制分配換取實體免稅」的交換邏輯。

Estate and Succession: Why Family Offices Hold CRE

遺產與傳承：為什麼家族辦公室偏好持有 CRE

Two structural features — both flagged as jurisdiction-dependent — help explain why multi-generational family capital gravitates toward direct real estate ownership more than most other asset classes. First, some systems apply a stepped-up basisA rule (present in some jurisdictions, not all) resetting an inherited asset's cost basis to its fair market value at the date of the original owner's death, potentially eliminating capital-gains tax on appreciation that occurred during their lifetime.部分（並非所有）司法轄區採行的規則，把繼承資產的成本基礎，重設為原業主過世當日的公允市價，可能因此消除原業主生前累積增值所應繳的資本利得稅。 rule at death: an heir's cost basis in an inherited property is reset to its current fair market value, rather than carrying forward the deceased's original (often much lower) cost basis. Where this applies, decades of unrealized, never-taxed appreciation from a long "exchange until death" holding pattern can pass to the next generation with the embedded gain effectively erased for capital-gains purposes — a very different outcome from selling the same property the day before death, which would trigger the full deferred gain.

有兩項結構性特徵——皆標註為依司法轄區而異——有助於解釋為什麼跨世代的家族資金，相對於大多數其他資產類別，更偏向直接持有不動產。第一，部分稅制在過世時適用成本基礎重設（stepped-up basis）A rule (present in some jurisdictions, not all) resetting an inherited asset's cost basis to its fair market value at the date of the original owner's death, potentially eliminating capital-gains tax on appreciation that occurred during their lifetime.部分（並非所有）司法轄區採行的規則，把繼承資產的成本基礎，重設為原業主過世當日的公允市價，可能因此消除原業主生前累積增值所應繳的資本利得稅。規則：繼承人取得繼承物件的成本基礎，會重設為當下的公允市價，而不是延續原業主原始（通常低得多）的成本基礎。適用這項規則的地方，經過數十年「交換到過世為止」持有模式所累積、從未實現也從未課稅的增值，可以在資本利得稅的意義上，實質上以增值被抹除的狀態傳給下一代——這跟在過世前一天出售同一筆物件、觸發全額遞延利得，是截然不同的結果。

Second, illiquid, hard-to-value assets like a large commercial property are, in some estate-tax regimes, eligible for valuation discounts (for lack of marketability or minority-interest positions when held through an entity) that reduce the taxable value of the estate — a lever unavailable to publicly traded, instantly-priced securities. Combined with leverage (a mortgaged property passes to heirs at its equity value, not its gross value, in most systems), these features are a documented reason multi-generational family offices concentrate wealth in direct CRE rather than liquid securities, alongside the more obvious motives of control and legacy. None of this is universal, automatic, or without its own compliance complexity — it is exactly the kind of structuring question that belongs in front of a qualified estate-tax advisor years before it becomes urgent, not after.

第二，像大型商用物件這類流動性低、難以評價的資產，在部分遺產稅制下，可能適用估值折讓（因缺乏市場性，或透過實體持有時屬於少數股權部位），藉此降低遺產的應稅價值——這是流動性高、隨時可定價的證券所沒有的槓桿。再結合槓桿效果（在多數稅制下，附有貸款的物件傳給繼承人時，是以股本價值而非總值計算），這些特徵是文獻記載的原因之一，說明為什麼跨世代家族辦公室會把財富集中在直接持有的 CRE，而非流動性證券，這還不包括控制權與傳承這類更明顯的動機。以上這些都不是普遍適用、自動生效、或毫無合規複雜性的——這正是應該在急迫之前的好幾年，就交給合格遺產稅顧問處理的架構問題，而不是等到事到臨頭。

Pitfall:常見陷阱： Structuring for tax efficiency before structuring for the deal's actual risk.
