## Gameplay and accounting

The deterministic model runs exactly once per turn. Playback takes 60 seconds for a month, 90 seconds for a season, with pause, 1×/2×/4× and direct settlement. Current routes and economic results are frozen during playback. Edits are queued for the following turn. Modals and hidden tabs suspend the simulation clock. Saves include current playback and queued plans; old saves migrate and resumed flights start paused.

Maintenance depot: $6M construction, $25k/month operations, 10-year depreciation, maintenance −30%. Lounge: $3M, $22k/month, 10 years, business appeal +8% for full service only. Fuel storage: $2M, $10k/month, 10 years, prebuy three months of 30% of unhedged fuel. Upfront fuel cash is not charged again when consumed. Costs, appeal and capacity are design values; only maintenance −30% follows the AirTycoon 4 reference.

Scenarios: open skies, 3.9% first-year profit, five-route connecting hub with 5% cumulative transfers, and ten-year shock survival. Progress uses settled results; achievement stamps grant no economic bonus.

## Release 18 — dispatch missions and a travel passport (2026-10-06)

The next playable loop is choose a short mission, review its suggested schedule, fly, collect settled rewards, then choose a new goal. The board offers up to three choices, based on reachable markets with profitable public forecasts. Missions cover first flights and delivered passengers, matching 70% of named rivals' seat supply while making a route profit, a profitable fleet without idle aircraft, cross-region connections, and loss reduction after a bad turn. Deadlines are at most three turns and shorten near game end. Players can play freely or abandon a mission; no cash penalty or cash reward is added.

`career.mjs` evaluates missions against actual settled route/company results, grants first-destination stamps, a one-time bonus for three cities per region, and five cosmetic airline levels. The board is generated by `model.careerBoard` from current route options and steady-state forecasts; it does not read future events. Suggested schedules remain editable. Mission delivery during playback is explicitly labelled a preview; completion and XP wait for settlement. Completion of a route mission does not imply a profitable company.

The travel passport keeps destination stamps across playthroughs and hubs in `tq-airline-passport`. Airline XP, mission progress and regional XP rewards belong to the current game and persist in its existing save. Legacy saves derive stamps from flown route statistics and resume normally; invalid career fields are reconstructed. The rival radar compares planned one-way seats with named game competitors' currently known schedules. Its seat bars exclude other real-market airlines and are labelled accordingly; they are not passenger shares.

Rewards do not change cash, fares, demand, competitor behaviour or costs. Existing five-hub/two-mode calibration bands and the teaching reports remain intact. `career.test.mjs` covers legal deterministic offers, delivery and profitability requirements, expiry and bankruptcy, one-time rewards, migration, full forecasted mission completion across all hubs/modes, and equality of economic reports with/without optional missions. `tests/career-browser.py` covers real mission clicks, editable plans, live previews, playback reload, cross-game stamps, both languages, muted/audible output and four viewport/theme variants.
