# Real city photographic horizons

Each asset below is a real photograph of the named city or its local geographic hinterland, independently verified against its Wikimedia Commons file page and license metadata on 2026-10-08. These photographs are not AI-generated. The game loads only the selected city photo.

The bundled WebP files are capped at 1600 pixels wide. Most are proportional size/encoding derivatives; Kobe is cropped to its upper 65% to retain Mount Maya and remove the foreground expressway. The manifest records every modification. Runtime rendering curves and softly blends the photograph at the distant horizon; it does not claim a surveyed road position or a current 2026 capture for older photographs. Each image and its displayed photographic derivative retain the license listed below. Photo licenses apply to the individual photo assets; the original game geometry and code are separate works.

Selected local sectors avoid duplicating the primary 3D landmarks: Taipei uses northern Yangmingshan hills; Kuala Lumpur uses the southwest Merdeka118 district; Kobe looks north toward Mount Maya and the Rokko ridge from the Port Tower itself; Gold Coast uses the landward Tamborine hinterland; Sydney uses the landward Barangaroo CBD; London uses the City skyline northeast of Westminster. The routes adapt geography to a compact closed circuit, so the photographed sector follows the actual land/water side of the modeled route. Kobe's original south-facing Meriken photograph was rejected because it created a second Port Tower, Maritime Museum and Meriken hotel. Three port/station alternatives were reviewed before a focused Rokko search found this daytime north-facing sector.

The city-specific original geographic silhouettes extend the photographic arc using photo-guided local features: Taipei basin and eastern hills; Kuala Lumpur foothills; Kobe’s Rokko ridge; the flat Thames basin; Sydney Harbour headlands; Gold Coast hinterland; Melbourne’s flat plain and distant Dandenong rise; Paris’s low basin; Prague’s west-bank hills; Newcastle’s Tyne valley; Bangkok’s delta plain; San Francisco’s bay hills; New York’s low harbor horizon; Vancouver’s North Shore mountains; Hanoi’s low lake skyline; Lisbon’s Tagus slopes; Marseille’s limestone hills; Nice’s coastal foothills; Warwick’s low wooded Avon valley. These silhouettes are original approximations, not terrain scans.

Photo placement is adjusted for each source composition. Bangkok's elevated foreground roofs sit below the distant horizon; Hanoi keeps its low Old Quarter roof line near ground level and the photographed lake below the visible band. Paris, Prague and Melbourne's elevated photographs keep their distant city roofs near the ground horizon. Warwick uses a narrow 13-degree sector so its church stays near a plausible distant height and its wooded valley stays low. Newcastle's close Quayside view uses a 15-degree sector to keep its church and offices at a modest distant scale. The photographs retain their aspect ratios rather than being stretched vertically. They do not become a floating wall of buildings or a second lake above the modeled street. Kobe's actual Mount Maya texture follows the north ridge while its southern water horizon stays open.

If a photograph fails to load, the game keeps that city’s own original geographic silhouette and never substitutes a generic image or another city’s view.
