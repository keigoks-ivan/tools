# Production vehicle references and original artwork

Checked 2026-10-09. The selectable garage contains 20 models listed by current manufacturer product pages, plus the previously supplied Ferrari 458 Italia clearly labelled **經典**. Availability, specification and trim names vary by market; a product page is not a promise of local dealer stock. BMW X3 M50 is the SUV requested by the user.

The 20 additional cars are original procedural game artwork in `../cars-production.js`, authored for the APEX project. No manufacturer CAD, scanned vehicle meshes, factory photographs, factory texture sheets or third-party car downloads are embedded in these additional models. Small illustrative marks are original vector artwork, not manufacturer badge assets. An original APEX closed-course plate and lettering atlas, plus the contact-shadow texture, are generated locally in code. Model names identify the vehicles being represented; this independent project claims no manufacturer affiliation or factory asset licence. The original 458 GLB remains covered by the separate attribution in `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

Each body uses its own longitudinal sections, cabin shape, axle placement, lights, intakes and aero details. Materials, tyre tread, wheels, fixed brake calipers, moving front steering and body load response are generated locally. The SL 63 and MX-5 have open passenger compartments, physical seats and dashboards; no opaque roof covers their seating positions. Geometry is an artistic interpretation based on the linked public specifications and manufacturer imagery, not engineering CAD. Body sections, overhangs, tyre choices and small components include approximations.

`../vehicles.mjs` preserves published exterior dimensions, converted to metres and rounded to the nearest millimetre where the source uses inches. Wheelbase is shared by the model and handling preset. Body width normally excludes mirrors. Ferrari publishes a 2,176 mm exterior width for 12Cilindri; the procedural body section uses an approximate 1,980 mm body width and adds mirrors rather than stretching the body to the published overall width. Other dimensions can vary with options, wheels, suspension, measurement convention or regional homologation. SL/MX-5 published heights describe the production vehicle; the modeled roofs are open.

Handling, torque curves, shift ratios, braking, tyre grip and sound settings are **game presets**, not certified manufacturer performance data. The current driving engine uses a simplified rear-drive bicycle model for every preset, including actual AWD and FWD vehicles. Prelude's in-game shift steps do not reproduce its production hybrid transmission. Audio is synthesized from engine type and cylinder count; it is not a factory engine recording. The purpose of the different presets is distinct playable acceleration, weight and turn response.

## Runtime detail and verification

Only the selected car is constructed. Additional production cars need no model or texture downloads and use the locally vendored Three.js library. The meshes are merged by material while preserving four separate steering/spin assemblies and atlas UVs. Each selected model has one generated 64×128 contact shadow; the 19 non-X3 models also have a single generated 256×128 mobile / 512×256 desktop rear artwork atlas. The X3 uses physical plate and roundel geometry. Switching cars disposes the previous model and its generated textures. Across these 20 models, the current desktop representation has 45,904–58,842 triangles; mobile has 27,854–34,746 triangles; the highest mesh count is 50. These counts describe this implementation, not a measured frame-rate guarantee.

`cars-production.test.mjs` checks all 20 models in both quality modes for finite geometry, road contact, axle spacing, steering, spin, brake lights, load response, bounded complexity and complete idempotent resource disposal. It also compares distinct painted silhouettes and casts rays onto the SL/MX-5 seat cushions to verify their passenger compartments are physically open. `vehicles.test.mjs` drives each selectable vehicle using inputs alone on each available circuit, checks valid laps and reset behavior, and compares acceleration/handling at controlled speeds. These checks establish numerical robustness and playable differences, not validation against physical vehicles.
